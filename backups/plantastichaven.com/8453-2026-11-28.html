Quick Answer: To propagate pothos (Epipremnum aureum), cut a healthy vine 1/4 inch below a swollen stem node (the small brown nub where leaves and aerial roots emerge). Single leaves without a stem node cannot grow into a new plant. Root the cuttings in clean water or moist potting mix in bright indirect light. Transfer water-rooted cuttings to soil once root branches reach 2 to 3 inches long, keeping the soil lightly damp for the first 14 days to ease root transition.

Golden pothos is the quintessential plant for learning propagation. Its stems are naturally packed with undifferentiated meristematic cells concentrated at each node, ready to produce vigorous root systems. Propagating allows you to turn leggy, sparse vines into full, bushy pots or share clones with fellow plant lovers.

Anatomy of a pothos cutting: where to cut for 100% success

The single most common beginner failure is cutting only a leaf petiole without stem tissue. A lone leaf in water can stay green for months via water uptake, but it lacks the axillary bud necessary to grow a new vine.

  • The Node: The swollen brown or dark green band along the main vine where the leaf petiole connects. This is where root primordia and dormant growth buds reside.
  • The Aerial Root Nub: A small, pimple-like brown bump located directly opposite or just below the node. These nubs rapidly elongate into functional roots upon contact with water or moist soil.
  • The Cut Point: Position clean, sharp bypass pruning shears or a razor blade exactly 1/4 to 1/2 inch below the node. Avoid leaving a long tail of empty internode stem, which will simply rot in water.
  • Leaf Retention: Each cutting should have 1 to 2 healthy leaves. Remove any lower leaf whose petiole would sit submerged below the waterline to prevent bacterial decomposition.

Step-by-step water propagation routine

  1. Take Clean Cuttings: Using sterilized shears, take 4 to 6 healthy single-node or two-node cuttings from actively growing vines. Multiplying several cuttings together produces a lush, full pot later.
  2. Submerge Nodes in Clean Vessel: Place cuttings into a clear glass jar or propagation tube. Fill with room-temperature filtered or tap water until the nodes and aerial root nubs are submerged, ensuring leaves remain elevated above the water.
  3. Provide Bright Indirect Light: Place the vessel on a warm windowsill receiving bright, filtered daylight. Avoid direct midday sunbeams, which heat the water and promote green algae blooms.
  4. Change Water Weekly: Replace the water every 5 to 7 days with fresh room-temperature water. This replenishes dissolved oxygen levels, which root cells need for cellular respiration.

Transferring water roots to soil: the potting-up criteria

Do not leave cuttings in water indefinitely. "Water roots" develop fragile, thin-walled cell structures adapted to an aquatic environment. If left in water for months, they struggle to develop the root hairs necessary to absorb water and nutrients from soil.

  • The Observable Potting Benchmark: Pot up cuttings when primary roots are 2 to 3 inches long and have begun forming secondary lateral root branches.
  • Pot Selection: Group 4 to 6 rooted cuttings together into a 4-inch or 5-inch container with drainage holes. A single cutting will look sparse; grouping cuttings creates an instantly full specimen.
  • The First 14-Day Transition Care: For the first 10 to 14 days after transplanting, keep the potting soil lightly, evenly damp (not waterlogged). This bridges the osmotic gap while water roots adapt to soil friction and fungal microflora. After two weeks, gradually transition to the standard dry-down routine outlined in our houseplant watering guide.

Frequently asked questions

Why are my pothos cuttings rotting in water?

Stems turning brown and mushy in water usually stem from three errors: leaving lower leaves submerged in water where they decay, taking cuttings with dull, unsterilized tools that crush vascular bundles, or failing to change stagnant water regularly, causing oxygen deprivation and anaerobic bacterial blooms.

Do I need rooting hormone to propagate pothos?

No. Pothos contains exceptionally high natural concentrations of endogenous auxins (rooting hormones). In fact, inserting a pothos cutting into water alongside other slow-rooting plant cuttings often speeds up root initiation in the companion plant.

Is pothos safe around pets during propagation?

No. Golden pothos contains insoluble calcium oxalate crystals and is toxic to cats and dogs. Keep open water propagation jars on secure high shelves where pets cannot tip them over or drink the water.
