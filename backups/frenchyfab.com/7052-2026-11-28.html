Quick answer: the safest homemade treats for French Bulldogs use a few plain ingredients, such as sweet potato, pumpkin, banana, oats, plain cooked chicken and xylitol-free peanut butter, with no salt, sugar, onion, garlic, grapes, chocolate or xylitol. Keep treats small, because VCA Animal Hospitals recommends that treats make up no more than 10% of a dog's daily calories, and Frenchies gain weight easily. Below are four simple recipes and the safety rules to follow.

Food-safety guidance comes from ASPCA Animal Poison Control and VCA Animal Hospitals, listed under Sources and checked on 25 September 2026. The recipes are simple home recipes, not veterinary diets; if your dog is on a prescription or elimination diet, ask your vet before giving any treat.

ASPCA Animal Poison Control also notes that milk and other dairy products can cause diarrhoea in pets, and that fatty foods and nuts can upset the stomach, so keep dairy and fat to a minimum.

Recipe 1: Baked sweet potato chews

Ingredients: 1 medium sweet potato.

  1. Heat the oven to about 120°C (250°F).
  2. Wash the sweet potato and slice it into thin rounds or strips, about 5 mm thick.
  3. Lay the slices on a lined baking tray and bake for 2.5 to 3 hours, turning halfway, until dry and chewy.
  4. Cool completely. Store in the fridge and use within about a week.

Recipe 2: Frozen pumpkin and banana bites

Ingredients: 120 g plain canned pumpkin purée (not pie filling, which contains sugar and spices), half a ripe banana, a splash of water.

  1. Mash the banana and mix it with the pumpkin and just enough water to make a smooth paste.
  2. Spoon into a small silicone mould or ice-cube tray.
  3. Freeze until solid. Give one small bite at a time, and keep them in the freezer.

Recipe 3: Peanut butter and oat biscuits

Ingredients: 120 g oat flour (or blended rolled oats), 2 tablespoons xylitol-free peanut butter, 1 egg, 2 to 4 tablespoons water.

  1. Heat the oven to 175°C (350°F).
  2. Mix the oat flour, peanut butter and egg, adding water a spoonful at a time until the dough holds together.
  3. Roll out to about 5 mm thick and cut into small shapes.
  4. Bake for 12 to 15 minutes until firm. Cool completely, store in an airtight container and use within about a week, or freeze.

Recipe 4: Plain chicken training bites

Ingredients: 1 boneless, skinless chicken breast.

  1. Poach or bake the chicken with no salt, oil, onion or seasoning until cooked through.
  2. Cool and cut into pea-sized pieces for training.
  3. Keep in the fridge and use within 3 days, or freeze in small portions.

How many treats can a French Bulldog have?

VCA Animal Hospitals recommends that treats and snacks make up no more than 10% of a dog's daily calories, with the rest from its main diet. For a small, low-energy breed like the French Bulldog, that is not much. Break treats into small pieces, use part of the daily kibble for training, and reduce meal portions slightly on days with lots of treats. Our feeding guide explains how to check body condition.

Treats for French Bulldog puppies

Puppies need a complete growth diet, so keep homemade treats very small and occasional, and introduce one new ingredient at a time. Pieces of the puppy's own food make excellent training treats.

Treats for Frenchies with allergies or sensitive stomachs

If your dog is on a food trial for allergies, even a single treat with a different protein can spoil the results. Ask your vet which treats fit the plan. If your dog gets diarrhoea, vomiting or itchy skin after a new treat, stop it and talk to your vet (see our food allergies guide).

If your dog eats something toxic

Note what and how much was eaten, and contact your vet or an animal poison control service straight away. ASPCA Animal Poison Control lists the foods above among the common people foods to keep away from pets. Our list of foods French Bulldogs cannot eat has more detail.

Frequently asked questions

What treats are good for French Bulldogs?

Small, low-calorie treats with simple ingredients, such as dried sweet potato, plain chicken or pieces of their regular food, work well.

Can French Bulldogs have peanut butter?

In small amounts, if it contains no xylitol and ideally no added salt or sugar. Check the label every time.

Can French Bulldogs eat jelly sweets or sugary snacks?

No. Sugary sweets add empty calories and some sugar-free sweets contain xylitol, which is toxic to dogs.
