💧 Water Propagation (Easiest Method)

Best for: Pothos, Philodendron, Monstera, Tradescantia, Spider Plant babies

  1. Cut a stem below a node (the bump where leaves grow)
  2. Remove leaves below the node
  3. Place in a jar of water (node submerged)
  4. Change water every 3-5 days
  5. Wait for roots to grow 2-3 inches (2-4 weeks)
  6. Plant in soil and keep moist for first week

🌱 Soil Propagation

Best for: Succulents, Snake Plant, ZZ Plant, Spider Plant babies

  1. Take a cutting or leaf
  2. Let it callous for 1-2 days (prevents rot)
  3. Plant in slightly moist soil
  4. Keep in bright indirect light
  5. Water sparingly until roots establish

✂️ Division

Best for: Peace Lily, Snake Plant, Spider Plant, Ferns

  1. Remove plant from pot
  2. Gently separate the root ball into sections
  3. Each section should have roots and leaves
  4. Plant each section in its own pot
  5. Water well and keep in shade for a few days

🪴 Propagation Cheat Sheet

  • Pothos: Water propagation, roots in 1-2 weeks
  • Monstera: Water propagation with aerial root, 3-4 weeks
  • Snake Plant: Leaf cuttings in soil, 4-8 weeks
  • Spider Plant: Babies in water or soil, 1-2 weeks
  • Succulents: Leaf on dry soil, 2-4 weeks
  • ZZ Plant: Leaf or stem in soil, 8-12 weeks (patience!)

🔧 Troubleshooting

  • Rotting in water? Change water more often, use clean jar
  • No roots after weeks? Check for node, try rooting hormone
  • Cutting dying? Too much direct light, move to indirect

🛒 Propagation Supplies Worth Owning

You can propagate most houseplants with a jar of water and a sharp knife. These four are the things that measurably raise your success rate on the harder species.

Highest impact

Rooting hormone

Best for woody and slow-rooting cuttings

  • Powder or gel
  • Speeds rooting
  • Reduces rot

Why it works: Auxin-based hormone shortens the window in which a cutting can rot before it roots. It makes little difference to pothos, which roots in water regardless, but a real difference to fiddle leaf fig, rubber plant and other stubborn woody stems.

Skip if: you only propagate pothos, tradescantia or spider plants — they do not need it.

Best for aroids

Sphagnum moss

Best medium for philodendron and monstera nodes

  • Holds moisture
  • Airy
  • Reusable

Why it works: Moss holds water while still letting air reach the node, which is the combination aroid roots want. Roots formed in moss transition into soil far more easily than water roots, which often collapse and have to be regrown.

Skip if: you are rooting succulents or cacti, which want to callus dry instead.

Best for success rate

Humidity dome or propagation box

Best for cuttings with no roots yet

  • Traps humidity
  • Clear lid
  • Stackable

Why it works: A cutting has no roots but still loses water through its leaves, and that imbalance is what kills most failed attempts. A closed environment cuts transpiration until roots can take over. A clear storage box with a lid works just as well as a purpose-made one.

Skip if: your home is already humid, or you are rooting in water where the leaves stay above the surface.

Best for rooting in water on display

  • Glass
  • Narrow neck
  • Wall or stand mounted

Why it works: A narrow neck holds the cutting at the right depth so the node sits in water while the leaves stay dry. Glass also lets you watch for cloudy water, which is the earliest sign that a cutting is rotting rather than rooting.
