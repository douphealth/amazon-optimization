Mice are one of the most common types of pests that people have to deal with in their homes. In addition to being a nuisance, mice can also pose some health risks to you and your family. One potential health concern that you should be aware of is the risk of developing a mice allergy.

A mouse allergy is an immune response to proteins in mouse urine, dander, and droppings. Exposure can trigger symptoms such as sneezing, itchy eyes, a runny nose, and sometimes asthma symptoms. If you suspect a mice allergy, see a doctor for help.

How Do You Know If You Have a Mouse Allergy?

If you suspect that you are allergic to mice, you should speak with a doctor. They can help you determine if a mouse allergy is causing your symptoms or if it’s something else.

If you are allergic to mice, you will likely experience symptoms after being exposed to mice or their dander. Some of the most common symptoms of a mouse allergy include:

• sneezing

• runny or stuffy nose

• itchy, red, or watery eyes

• coughing or wheezing

• chest tightness or shortness of breath

• hives or a rash

• swelling or itching of the skin

• Nausea or vomiting is nonspecific and does not establish a mouse allergy.

• Severe breathing difficulty, fainting, or a suspected severe allergic reaction requires emergency care; this is not a mouse-specific diagnostic sign.

If you think you may be experiencing anaphylaxis, seek emergency medical attention immediately.

What Causes Mouse Allergies?

Mice allergies are caused by exposure to mice urine and dander. Dander is the tiny flakes of skin that mice and other animals shed.

Urine and dander from mice can get into the air and spread through your home. This is especially true if you have a mice infestation. Ridding your home of mice, and cleaning up the urine and dander, can help reduce your risk of exposure.

Other ways you can be exposed to mice urine and dander include:

• touching surfaces contaminated with urine and dander

• eating or drinking contaminated food or water

• breathing in contaminated dust

• being bitten by a mouse

• touching a mouse

• handling their urine or dander

What Can I Do to Keep Mice Out of My Home?

While you can’t live in a bubble to avoid mouse allergens, there are steps you can take to minimize your exposure. Here are some ways to prevent mice from getting into your home:

• Keep your kitchen clean and store food in airtight containers.

• Seal cracks and holes on the outside of your home, especially around the foundation, doors, and windows.

• Store firewood away from the building and keep it raised; adapt the location to the site and local guidance.

• Keep your garbage in a sealed container and take it out regularly.

• Discuss ventilation and other allergen-control measures with a clinician; a humidity target does not diagnose or remove mouse allergens. Do not use airflow to disturb dry rodent waste.

• Get rid of any standing water around your home, and fix leaky pipes and faucets.

• Keep your yard trimmed and free of tall grass and weeds.

If you’re allergic to mice, you should also:

• Avoid entering or disturbing contaminated areas. Ask qualified professionals about exposure precautions; a mask alone does not establish safety.

• Wash your hands and change your clothes after working in areas where mice may have been present.

• Do not vacuum rodent urine, droppings or nests, even with a HEPA vacuum. Wet-clean contamination using CDC guidance; discuss other routine allergen control with a clinician.

• Consider hiring a professional to remove mice from your home.

How Can I Get Rid of a Mouse Allergy?

If you suspect that you have a mouse allergy, it’s important to get tested as soon as possible. Your doctor can determine whether your symptoms are caused by mouse exposure or something else.

Reducing mouse-allergen exposure can support symptom management but does not guarantee that an allergy disappears. Work with a clinician on diagnosis and treatment.

A clinician may use skin or blood tests alongside the history and examination. A test result alone does not establish that mice caused a particular symptom.

What’s the Best Way to Get Rid of Mice?

If you’re allergic to mice, it’s best to avoid them altogether. However, this is easier said than done. Mice are common household pests, and an infestation can be difficult to avoid.

To help reduce your risk of exposure to mice, follow these tips:

• Keep your home clean and clutter-free

• Store food in airtight containers

• Take out the trash regularly

• Seal up any cracks or holes in your home

• Keep your yard tidy and free of debris

If you’re allergic to mice, it’s important to take steps to reduce your risk of exposure. Be sure to talk to your doctor about other ways to manage your allergy.

Conclusion

The best way to avoid mice allergies is to avoid exposure to mice. Keep your home clean and store food in airtight containers. If you suspect a mouse infestation, call a professional exterminator.
