The Novablast has always been ASICS’s fun shoe — the one in the lineup that prioritized bounce over business. For five generations it did that with a single job description: soft, springy, a little bit wild. The Novablast 6 changes the recipe in the one place that matters most. ASICS has dropped its premium FF TURBO SQUARED foam — the stuff previously reserved for the flagship Megablast — into a forefoot “trampoline pod,” sitting it on top of the familiar FF BLAST MAX base. The result is the first dual-foam Novablast, and the first one with proper ASICSGRIP traction rubber.

That sounds like marketing. It isn’t, quite. This is the update Novablast 5 owners actually asked for: the old shoe’s wet-surface grip was its one widely agreed weakness, and the forefoot is now genuinely more energetic than the heel. The trade-off, which several reviewers have flagged, is that the new forefoot pod is noticeable — you feel it working — and the tall, soft platform asks a little more of your ankles than a traditional trainer.

🔧 Two foams, one shoe — here’s what FF BLAST MAX and FF TURBO SQUARED actually do when your foot hits the ground, and why ASICS split the midsole in two.

Technology deep-dive: what the foams actually do

FF BLAST MAX (heel and base). This is ASICS’s energetic everyday foam — the same family that powered the Novablast 5. Its job here is the landing: soft, protective, and consistent over long runs. Keeping it in the heel was a deliberate choice — the heel is where most daily miles get absorbed, and it is proven there.

FF TURBO SQUARED (forefoot pod). This is the interesting half. FF TURBO is the super-foam from ASICS’s premium racers and the Megablast; the “SQUARED” version is molded into a trampoline-style pod under the forefoot. The idea: the forefoot compresses and springs back with more energy than standard foam, so toe-off feels snappier without a carbon plate. Reviewers consistently report that you can feel the pod — it is not subtle. For runners who like a lively forefoot, that is the whole point. For runners who like a uniform ride, it is the thing to test on a short run first.

ASICSGRIP + AHAR LO (outsole). ASICSGRIP is ASICS’s high-traction rubber compound, placed in the forefoot where push-off grip matters most; AHAR LO (a lower-hardness, durable rubber) covers the heel and high-wear zones. On paper this is the single biggest functional upgrade over the 5, and it is the reason the 6 is a meaningfully better wet-weather and dusty-path shoe than its predecessor.

Upper. The engineered woven construction cuts down on overlays, which keeps weight down and breathability up. The tongue wings wrap the midfoot to stop the tongue migrating mid-run, and the collar is generously padded — one recurring reviewer note is that the heel area can run warm, so hot-climate runners should know the forefoot breathes better than the collar.

Fit and sizing

The consistent signal from reviewers who have actually worn the shoe — including a four-runner test panel — is true to size, the same size as past Novablast models. The midfoot lockdown from the tongue wings is secure without being aggressive, and a wide version exists for broader feet. As with any tall-stack trainer, give the forefoot pod one short easy run before committing to a long run, and buy from a retailer with a sane return policy in case the pod feel is not for you.

FAQ

How much does the ASICS Novablast 6 cost?

The Novablast 6 launched in July 2026 at $155 in the US, £140 in the UK, and €160 in Europe. That is $5 more than the Novablast 5’s launch price — the premium covers the FF TURBO SQUARED forefoot pod and the ASICSGRIP outsole upgrade.

What is the Novablast 6’s stack height and drop?

Men’s: 41.5 mm heel / 33.5 mm forefoot with an 8 mm drop. Women’s: 40.5 / 32.5 mm, same 8 mm drop. It is 1 mm taller than the Novablast 5 at both ends.

What foam does the Novablast 6 use?

A dual-foam setup: FF BLAST MAX forms the base and heel for soft landings, while an FF TURBO SQUARED trampoline pod sits in the forefoot for a snappier toe-off. It is the first Novablast to use FF TURBO SQUARED, a foam previously reserved for ASICS’s premium models like the Megablast.

How much does the Novablast 6 weigh?

About 8.8 oz / 249 g in a men’s shoe and roughly 7.7 oz / 215 g in a women’s shoe. Independent measurements vary a little by size (some men’s samples weighed 252–262 g), and it is lighter than the ~9 oz Novablast 5 despite the taller stack.

Does the Novablast 6 run true to size?

Yes — reviewers who have worn it report true-to-size fit, consistent with past Novablast models. The tongue-wing construction gives secure midfoot lockdown, and a wide version is available for broader feet.

What is the difference between the Novablast 6 and Novablast 5?

Four things: (1) the 6 adds an FF TURBO SQUARED forefoot pod for a livelier toe-off; (2) the outsole upgrades to ASICSGRIP forefoot rubber + AHAR LO heel, fixing the 5’s wet-traction weakness; (3) it is lighter (~8.8 oz vs ~9 oz) on a slightly taller stack; (4) it costs $5 more ($155 vs $150). The 5 remains a good buy on discount if grip was never an issue for you.

Is the Novablast 6 good for marathon training?

Yes, in the right role: it is built for the easy miles and long runs that make up most of marathon training. It is a max-cushion daily trainer, not a racer — pair it with a plated shoe for workouts and race day if you are training seriously.

Is the Novablast 6 stable enough for overpronators?

It is a neutral shoe on a tall, soft platform, so it is not a stability shoe. Some reviewers note a slight inward wobble from the soft forefoot pod. Mild overpronators who have done fine in neutral trainers may be okay, but anyone who needs real guidance should look at stability models instead.

How is the grip on wet roads?

Meaningfully better than the Novablast 5. The 6 uses ASICSGRIP rubber in the forefoot with AHAR LO in the heel and high-wear zones — a direct response to the traction complaints about the previous model. It is still a road shoe, not a trail shoe, so treat painted lines and wet corners with respect.

Novablast 6 vs Nike Pegasus 42 — which should I buy?

Buy the Novablast 6 ($155) if you want maximum bounce and the lightest shoe — it is the more fun, more cushioned ride. Buy the Pegasus 42 ($145) if you want the traditional workhorse: lower stack (37/27 mm), a firmer and more connected feel, full-length Air Zoom, and $10 left in your pocket.
