The stages of the heat cycle

A dog's reproductive (estrous) cycle has several stages. Owners usually notice only the first two, when there is discharge and males start paying attention.

StageWhat you will seeWhat it means
ProestrusA swollen vulva and a bloody discharge. She attracts males but usually will not accept them yet.The start of "heat". Bloody discharge is often the first sign owners notice, but it can appear several days in.
EstrusThe discharge thins and turns watery or pink. She may stand for, or seek out, a male.The receptive, fertile phase: generally about 9 days, with a range of 2 to 24 days.
DiestrusDischarge stops and interest from males fades.Hormones stay high whether or not she is pregnant. False pregnancy and pyometra (below) belong to this period.
AnestrusNothing visible.The resting phase before the next cycle.

Signs your French Bulldog is in heat

  • Swelling of the vulva. This is the earliest sign, but it is not always obvious.
  • Vaginal discharge that starts bloody and becomes thinner, watery and pink as the cycle goes on. The amount varies from dog to dog.
  • More frequent urination or marking, indoors or on walks. Her urine carries pheromones that signal her state to other dogs.
  • Attention from male dogs, sometimes from a surprising distance.

How often, and from what age?

The age of the first heat varies with size and individual development; a breed average is not a reliable date for an individual French Bulldog. Smaller breeds tend to cycle earlier than large breeds. Most dogs then come into heat about twice a year, and some small dogs cycle three times a year. Early cycles are often irregular: it can take up to two years for a young female to settle into a regular pattern, so an uneven gap between her first two heats is not unusual.

When can she get pregnant?

A female usually ovulates around the time the discharge turns watery, which is her most fertile point. She can still conceive at any point during estrus, because sperm can survive in the reproductive tract for about a week, and a "tie" is not required for pregnancy. Pregnancy lasts about 63 days (nine weeks).

If a planned mating is being arranged, ask your vet how they will time ovulation; the discharge alone is not a precise guide. If an accidental mating happens, contact your vet as soon as possible: there are options, and each carries risks your vet will explain.

Why breeding a French Bulldog is a medical decision

French Bulldogs are among the breeds that most often need help to give birth. In a UK survey of more than 22,000 litters across 151 breeds, over 80% of French Bulldog litters were delivered by caesarean section, one of the three highest rates recorded (Evans & Adams, 2010). Anyone considering a litter should plan the pregnancy, the delivery and the anaesthetic with a vet from the start. Our French Bulldog C-section guide explains what that planning involves.

Looking after her during a heat

  • Keep her on a lead on every walk and supervise her in the garden. Avoid dog parks and off-lead areas until the cycle is over.
  • Keep intact males away, including dogs she lives with. Related dogs will mate.
  • Use washable season pants or dog diapers if discharge is marking furniture. Change them often and check the skin underneath, especially around skin folds. They manage discharge; they do not replace separation from intact males.
  • Keep walks short and cool. Heat doesn't change her breathing, but French Bulldogs are brachycephalic and overheat easily, so the usual overheating precautions still apply.

When to call the vet

  • Signs of pyometra in the weeks after a heat: lethargy, poor appetite, drinking and urinating more, vomiting, a pus-like or bloody discharge, or a swollen abdomen. Pyometra is a bacterial infection of the uterus in unspayed females. It is reported mainly in dogs over five years old and tends to occur 4 to 6 weeks after estrus. It can progress rapidly to shock and death, so treat these signs as an emergency. A closed-cervix pyometra produces no discharge at all.
  • Heat signs lasting more than 40 days, which can point to follicular cysts on the ovary.
  • Signs of false pregnancy that distress her: weight gain, swollen mammary glands, milk, nesting or "mothering" toys. This is common at the end of a cycle and usually settles in 1 to 3 weeks. Don't milk the glands; that stimulates more milk.
  • Any accidental mating.

Spaying

Spaying is the most reliable way to prevent pregnancy and pyometra. Many vets recommend it before the first heat, because the first cycle is hard to predict. Timing is a conversation to have with your own vet. For a French Bulldog, that conversation should also cover how the clinic manages anaesthesia and recovery in flat-faced breeds.

Frequently asked questions

How long is a French Bulldog in heat?

Visible heat signs average about two to three weeks in dogs, although individual cycles vary. This includes the early proestrus phase and the receptive estrus phase. Receptivity alone generally averages about 9 days but can vary widely; do not use a calendar or the end of bleeding to assume mating is safe. See VCA’s explanation of cycle duration and mating.

Can a French Bulldog get pregnant on her first heat?

Yes. Dogs can become pregnant on their very first cycle, which is one reason vets often suggest spaying before it.

How often do French Bulldogs go into heat?

Usually about twice a year. Small dogs sometimes cycle three times a year, and young dogs can be irregular for up to two years.
