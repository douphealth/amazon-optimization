Quick answer: fish oil (omega-3 fatty acids, mainly EPA and DHA) is the supplement with the best evidence for dogs. It can help dogs with osteoarthritis and is one part of managing allergic skin disease, both relevant to French Bulldogs. The right amount depends on your dog's weight, diet and health, and products vary a lot in how much EPA and DHA they contain, so agree the product and dose with your vet rather than following a generic chart, especially for puppies.

This guide summarises published veterinary research listed under Sources, checked on 25 September 2026. It is general education, not veterinary advice, and it intentionally does not give doses.

Omega-3 for French Bulldogs at a glance

Joints and mobility

French Bulldogs can develop joint problems such as hip dysplasia and are prone to spinal disc disease. For arthritis-type pain, omega-3 from fish oil has the strongest evidence among supplements. In a randomised, double-blinded trial of 38 dogs with osteoarthritis, dogs fed a diet containing fish-oil omega-3 for 90 days showed a significant improvement in weight bearing. A 2022 systematic review and meta-analysis of 72 trials found clear pain-relief evidence for omega-3-enriched diets and omega-3 supplements, while glucosamine-chondroitin products showed a marked lack of effect.

Omega-3 is a support, not a diagnosis. Limping, a hunched back or reluctance to jump can be signs of disc disease, which needs prompt veterinary care. See our hip dysplasia guide.

Skin and allergies

Itchy skin and recurrent ear infections are common in French Bulldogs. The International Committee on Allergic Diseases of Animals (ICADA) guidelines for atopic dermatitis describe treatment as multifaceted: finding and avoiding flare factors, bathing, controlling itch with effective medications, and possibly increasing essential fatty acid intake. Fish oil can support the skin, but a dog that is scratching, licking its paws or getting repeated ear infections needs a vet plan. See our food allergies guide.

Choosing a fish oil

  • Look at the EPA and DHA content, not the total "fish oil" figure. Products differ widely, which is why doses need to be worked out for the specific product.
  • Choose a product made for dogs with a lot number, expiry date and clear dosing instructions.
  • Check whether your dog's food already contains added omega-3; therapeutic joint and skin diets often do, and your vet will factor that in.
  • Store it as directed. Oils go rancid; a strong rancid smell means it is time to replace it.
  • Never use human gummies or chewables; some human supplements contain xylitol, which is toxic to dogs.

Safety

  • Introduce it gradually; loose stools, a fishy smell and extra calories are the usual downsides.
  • Tell your vet before surgery and if your dog takes any other medication.
  • Count the calories, especially if your Frenchie needs to lose weight (see how to help an overweight Frenchie).

Frequently asked questions

How much fish oil should I give my French Bulldog?

It depends on the product's EPA and DHA content, your dog's weight and diet, and why you are giving it. Ask your vet to calculate a dose for the specific product rather than using a generic chart.

Can I give fish oil to an 8-month-old French Bulldog puppy?

Check with your vet first. Puppies on a complete growth diet usually get the essential fats they need from their food, and extra fat and calories can upset the balance.

What are the benefits of omega-3 for French Bulldogs?

The best evidence is for easing osteoarthritis pain. It can also be part of a vet-led plan for allergic skin.
