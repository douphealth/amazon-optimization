Heart-Rate Accuracy and HRV: Why a Chest Strap Still Matters

The biggest reason to buy HRM 600 is not that it shows heart rate. Many watches already do that. The reason is that chest-based measurement is usually more stable during the exact workouts where wrist sensors can struggle: hard intervals, changing intensity, cold starts, gripping handlebars, strength training, and races.

Wrist sensors can lag when your intensity changes quickly. A chest strap sits closer to the heart’s electrical signal, so it can be a stronger choice when training zones, threshold work, and pacing decisions need cleaner input.

Running Dynamics and Step Speed Loss Explained

HRM 600 becomes far more valuable when paired with a compatible Garmin watch. It can unlock advanced running dynamics such as stride length, vertical oscillation, ground contact time balance, and Step Speed Loss. These metrics help you evaluate how your form changes as pace, fatigue, shoes, terrain, and workout type change.

What Is Step Speed Loss?

Step Speed Loss estimates how much speed you lose when your foot contacts the ground while running. Lower is typically better because it suggests you need less propulsive work to regain speed after ground contact.

How to Use Step Speed Loss Without Overthinking It

  • Track trends, not single runs. Terrain, fatigue, shoes, pace, and weather can all affect the number.
  • Compare similar sessions. A flat easy run should not be compared directly with a hilly tempo run.
  • Use it with cadence and ground contact time. One running metric rarely tells the whole story.
  • Make small changes. If you are adjusting cadence, use our running cadence guide and avoid forcing a dramatic stride change overnight.

Step Speed Loss does not make you faster by itself. It gives you feedback. The training still comes from consistent running, smart progression, strength work, recovery, and appropriate pacing.

How to Set Up the Garmin HRM 600

  1. Charge it first. Do not start your first workout with an unknown battery level.
  2. Moisten the strap electrodes. This improves contact before you sweat.
  3. Wear it snugly. Place it below the chest muscles with the Garmin logo facing outward.
  4. Wake the module. Use the button and LED status to confirm it is active.
  5. Pair it correctly. Use Garmin Connect or your Garmin watch sensor menu instead of only your phone’s Bluetooth settings.
  6. Start the right activity. Use your watch for normal workouts or Garmin Connect for supported watch-free recording.
  7. Clean it after training. Remove the module, rinse the strap, and follow Garmin’s care guidance.

Troubleshooting Dropouts

  • Wet the electrodes before starting.
  • Tighten the strap slightly if it moves during harder efforts.
  • Clean salt and sweat residue from the strap.
  • Make sure the module is charged.
  • Pair through the correct Garmin menu.
  • Keep firmware updated in Garmin Connect.

Is the Garmin HRM 600 worth it?

Which Garmin HRM 600 size should I buy?

Does the Garmin HRM 600 work with non-Garmin devices?

It can transmit heart-rate data over ANT+ and Bluetooth Low Energy, so basic compatibility is broad. However, advanced Garmin features such as Step Speed Loss and Garmin-specific running dynamics require compatible Garmin devices.

Does HRM 600 measure Step Speed Loss?

Yes. Step Speed Loss is one of the headline HRM 600 features, but you need a compatible Garmin watch to view and use it.

Can Garmin HRM 600 record without a watch?

Yes. HRM 600 can record selected activities when a watch is not practical and sync data to Garmin Connect.

Can you swim with the Garmin HRM 600?

Yes. HRM 600 has a 5 ATM water rating and can store heart-rate data during swim activities, then forward it to a compatible Garmin smartwatch after saving the activity.

Is Garmin HRM 600 better than HRM-Pro Plus?

HRM 600 is better if you want Step Speed Loss, a rechargeable battery, a detachable module, and expanded watch-free recording. HRM-Pro Plus may be better if you prefer a CR2032 battery and find it at a lower price.

Is Garmin HRM 600 better than Polar H10?

For Garmin-specific training metrics, yes. For universal app compatibility and value outside Garmin, Polar H10 remains a strong alternative.

How long does Garmin HRM 600 battery last?

Garmin rates HRM 600 for up to 2 months when used 1 hour per day. Real-world battery life depends on use frequency, temperature, storage, and connection behavior.
