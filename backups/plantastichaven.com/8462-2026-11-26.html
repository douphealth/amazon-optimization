Monstera · Diagnosis · Yellow Leaves

Quick answer: Monstera leaves usually turn yellow because roots stayed wet too long, drainage is poor, the plant dried too hard, light is too weak, or an older lower leaf is naturally aging. Check how many leaves are yellow, whether the soil is wet or dry, and whether new growth still looks strong before you cut or water.

Normal yellowing versus a problem

A mature Monstera can shed an older lower leaf. That is normal if the rest of the plant is firm and new growth is healthy. Spreading yellowing, soft stems, pests, or wet soil is different.

Why overwatering is common

Overwatering usually means watering too often for the pot, soil, and light. A dense mix in low light can stay wet long enough to stress roots even if the owner did not water aggressively.

Why light matters

Low light slows growth and water use. It can also produce smaller, paler leaves. Fixing light often makes future leaves stronger, but old yellow leaves will not become green again.

Step-by-step practical instructions

Use this sequence when you first notice yellow Monstera leaves.

Identify the pattern

Note whether one old leaf or many leaves are yellowing.

Check soil moisture

Feel the upper and middle mix, not just the surface.

Inspect drainage

Confirm the pot drains and no water is trapped in a cachepot.

Check roots if needed

If soil is wet and yellowing is spreading, slide the root ball out gently.

Improve light

Move to bright indirect light if growth is pale, small, or slow.

Prune only spent leaves

Remove fully yellow or damaged leaves with clean snips.

Wait for new growth

Judge recovery by future leaves, not old damaged foliage.

Common mistakes and troubleshooting

Treating all yellowing as thirst

Wet roots can make leaves yellow and droop too.

Fertilizing immediately

Stressed roots cannot use fertilizer well.

Repotting repeatedly

Too many changes can compound stress.

Ignoring pests

Check undersides and stems if yellowing includes speckling, sticky residue, or webbing.

Troubleshooting rule: Change one variable at a time, then watch new growth. Old damaged leaves may not repair themselves, but the plant should stabilize and produce healthier growth once the root cause is fixed.

Pet safety, toxicity, and household-risk notes

Pet safety: Monstera contains irritating calcium oxalate crystals and is commonly listed as toxic to cats and dogs if chewed. Keep it out of reach and call a veterinarian or poison hotline if a pet bites or swallows plant tissue.

Yellow leaves that fall or are pruned should be discarded where pets cannot chew them.

FAQ

Should I cut yellow Monstera leaves off?

Cut fully yellow, dying, or damaged leaves. If the leaf is partly green and firm, you can wait while fixing the cause.

Can yellow Monstera leaves turn green again?

Usually no. Focus on correcting the cause so future growth is healthy.

Does yellow mean overwatering?

Often, but not always. Check soil moisture, drainage, light, pests, and recent changes.

Why are lower Monstera leaves yellowing?

One old lower leaf may be normal aging. Multiple lower leaves yellowing together often suggests root or light stress.

Can pests cause yellow Monstera leaves?

Yes. Spider mites, scale, mealybugs, and thrips can cause yellowing, speckling, or general decline.
