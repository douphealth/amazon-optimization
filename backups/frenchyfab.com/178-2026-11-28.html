Quick answer: the best games for French Bulldogs work the brain more than the lungs: sniffing and "find it" games, food puzzles, short trick-training sessions, gentle tug and hide-and-seek. The Kennel Club suggests up to an hour of exercise a day for the breed, and flat-faced dogs overheat easily, so keep active games short, play at cool times of day, and stop at the first sign of heavy panting.

Why brain games suit French Bulldogs

French Bulldogs are brachycephalic, meaning their shortened airway makes it harder to cool down. The Royal Veterinary College lists flat-faced breeds such as French Bulldogs among the animals most susceptible to heatstroke, and notes that exercising in warm weather can be enough to trigger it. Games that use the nose and brain tire a Frenchie out without the long runs that risk overheating. They also help with boredom: the ASPCA notes that chewing and other unwanted behaviours often come from boredom, so regular mental exercise can reduce problems at home.

Six games to play with your French Bulldog

1. Find it

Ask your dog to wait, place a few treats in easy view, then say "find it" and let it search. Gradually hide them behind furniture, under cups or in another room. Use pieces of your dog's daily food to avoid extra calories.

2. Snuffle mats and food puzzles

Scatter kibble in a snuffle mat or use a puzzle feeder for meals. It turns eating into a game, slows down fast eaters and keeps your dog busy while you are out of sight.

3. Trick training

Teach one simple trick at a time, such as spin, touch (nose to hand) or paw, using small rewards and a marker word like "yes". Keep sessions to a few minutes and finish while your dog is still keen. Our French Bulldog training tips explain the basics of reward-based training.

4. Gentle tug

Tug is fine for most dogs if it has rules: your dog lets go on "drop it", play pauses if teeth touch skin, and you keep the game low and gentle. Avoid lifting your dog off the ground or jerking the toy, which can strain the neck and back.

5. Hide-and-seek

While someone holds your dog, hide in an easy spot and call once. Reward generously when your dog finds you. It is a fun way to practise recall indoors.

6. Short, low-key fetch

A few gentle throws on soft grass are fine for a healthy adult Frenchie. Avoid long repeated sprints, jumping to catch toys and play on slippery floors, which put strain on the back and joints.

Playing safely

  • Watch the breathing. Stop and rest if your dog pants heavily, breathes noisily, drools a lot or slows down. The RVC lists these among early signs of heatstroke; see our overheating guide.
  • Play at cool times of day in warm weather, and keep water nearby.
  • Protect the back: discourage jumping off furniture and use rugs on slippery floors.
  • Choose toys sized for a Frenchie and supervise chewing; replace toys that break into pieces.
  • Count treats as part of your dog's daily food so games do not lead to weight gain (see our feeding guide).

Frequently asked questions

What games do French Bulldogs like to play?

Most enjoy sniffing and treat-search games, food puzzles, gentle tug and short training sessions. Watch what your dog gets excited about and build on it.

How long should I play with my French Bulldog?

Several short sessions a day work better than one long one. The Kennel Club suggests up to an hour of exercise a day in total, including walks.

Are games good for a bored Frenchie left alone?

A food puzzle or long-lasting chew can help your dog settle while you are out. If your dog is distressed when alone, see our separation anxiety guide.
