Finding the Right Fit

One of the most important considerations when selecting Nike running shorts is choosing the right fit. Nike offers shorts in a range of fits to suit different body types and running styles.

Loose Fit

For runners who prefer a relaxed feel, Nike has shorts with a loose fit through the hips and thighs. This allows for a full range of motion without restriction. The loose fit is great if you dislike the compression of tighter-fitting shorts. Just be aware that excess fabric can slow you down.

Compression Fit

At the other end of the spectrum are Nike’s compression shorts. These are made from elastic fabrics that hug the body closely. The tight fit keeps muscles stable and can improve circulation. Compression shorts also minimize chafing. Some runners feel energized by the gentle pressure. But the snugness may feel restrictive to some.

Mid-Rise

Most Nike running shorts sit at mid-hip level. This mid-rise height provides good coverage for modesty while allowing free leg movement. Shorts that hit mid-hip also look flattering on most body types. This versatile mid-rise fit suits most runners.

High Rise vs Low Rise

Nike has high-rise shorts extending towards the natural waist, as well as low-rise options sitting on the hips. High-rise shorts offer more coverage, while low-rise prioritizes freedom of motion. Choose based on personal modesty preferences and comfort.

Understanding Nike’s fit options will help you select shorts aligned with your needs. Try both loose and compression fits to see what you like best for different workouts and races. Most runners end up reaching for mid-rise shorts most often for their balance of coverage and mobility.

Fabrics and Features

Nike is renowned for its innovative performance fabrics and functional features that enhance your run. These technologies make Nike shorts stand out from the competition.

Dri-FIT Fabric

Nike’s proprietary Dri-FIT fabric is exceptionally breathable and moisture-wicking. It pulls sweat away from your skin to the outer layer where it evaporates quickly. This keeps you dry, cool, and comfortable even during intense workouts. The featherweight fabric also allows maximum airflow.

Mesh Panels

Strategically placed mesh panels provide targeted ventilation. High heat and sweat zones like the back of the knees benefit from extra breathability. Mesh adds ventilation without sacrificing coverage.

Side Pockets

Many Nike shorts incorporate side pockets to hold personal items. Choose between open pockets or zippered for security. Side pockets are great for stashing gels, keys or an ID card. Just ensure any items are positioned snugly to avoid bouncing.

Inner Liners

Higher-end Nike shorts feature a built-in inner liner for chafe-free comfort. The lightweight liner provides an extra layer between the skin and fabric to minimize irritation. Integrated liners also eliminate the need to wear underwear.

Reflective Details

For safe runs in low light, some Nike shorts have reflective trims like logos or piping. These reflective accents help enhance visibility in dawn, dusk, or nighttime conditions.

Nike’s advanced fabrics and functional features give their running shorts a performance edge. Consider your needs like pocket storage or chafing protection to choose shorts with the ideal attributes.

Length and Style

Nike running shorts come in lengths ranging from ultra-short 2-3 inches to longer 5-7 inch styles hitting mid-thigh. Length impacts coverage, range of motion, and aesthetics. Style differences like curved hems and split side seams also affect look and function.

Shorter Lengths

Nike’s shortest running shorts measure 2-3 inches to provide maximum freedom of movement. The minimal coverage is ideal for runners who don’t worry about modesty. However, the ultra-short lengths can cause chafing for some. Use body glide or liners to prevent irritation.

Longer Lengths

At the other end of the spectrum, Nike has 5-7 inch running shorts that hit mid-thigh. The extra length offers more coverage for modesty. But some runners feel restricted by longer shorts, especially when sprinting or hill training. Save longer lengths for easy runs.

Curved Hem

Many Nike shorts feature a curved hem that scoops down lower in the back of the legs. This tailored shape provides a streamlined look that flatters your natural curves. Curved hems also allow greater stride length.

Split Side Hem

Some Nike shorts have a split side seam creating an angled hem. This allows even more freedom of movement to power up hills or pick up speed. The split hem style adds visual interest too.

Consider your preferred balance of coverage, mobility, and style when choosing a short length. Tailored details like curved and split hems boost aesthetic appeal while enabling free movement.

Factors to Consider When Buying

When shopping for Nike running shorts, keep the following factors in mind to select the best pair for your needs:

Climate and Weather

Consider the climate where you’ll wear the shorts most. In hot humid weather, choose ultra-lightweight, breathable fabrics like Nike Dri-FIT that wick moisture. Cooler temps may require more coverage from longer 5-7” inseams.

Budget

Nike offers running shorts at good, better, and best price points. Entry-level models have the basics covered, while high-end shorts feature premium details. Set a budget and look for sales to get more value.

Activity Type

Choose ultra-short lengths and compression fits for racing and speed workouts. Training runs and longer miles may prioritize comfort and coverage in a mid-length short.

Size and Fit

Consult Nike’s size chart and measure your waist and hips. Their shorts fit true to size. Trying styles on is ideal to assess fit. Order a few sizes if between sizes or unsure.

Fabric Care

Most Nike shorts can be machine-washed cold and tumble-dried low. Avoid fabric softeners and bleach. Some premium fabrics require air drying though. Check the care label.

Considering these factors will help you select high-performing Nike running shorts tailored to your specific needs and preferences.
