How We Picked These Watches

We didn't just sort by price. Every watch on this list had to clear four non-negotiable bars for marathon beginners:

  1. Built-in GPS with 15+ hours of battery. The 2× rule (explained below) means a 5-hour finisher needs ~10 hours of rated GPS. We set the floor at 15 to leave real margin.
  2. Wrist-based heart rate. Zone-based training is how beginners build aerobic fitness without burning out. No HR sensor, no recommendation.
  3. A real training ecosystem. Free coaching plans (Garmin Coach, COROS training plans) or a best-in-class app platform (Apple). A watch that just records data without guiding you is a missed opportunity for a first-timer.
  4. Current-generation models. Every pick is a 2026-current model you can actually buy new today — no discontinued watches, no "great if you find it refurbished" compromises.

Your First 4 Weeks With a Marathon Watch

Buying the watch is step one. Here's how to actually use it:

1

Week 1: Wear it 24/7

Don't even run yet — just wear it. The watch needs baseline data: resting heart rate, sleep patterns, HRV. This baseline is what makes the coaching features smart later.

2

Week 2: Start a Garmin Coach / training plan

Pick your race date and goal in the app. The plan adapts to your fitness — trust the easy days. Most beginners run their easy runs too fast; the watch's zone alerts fix that.

3

Week 3: Learn your zones

Do the watch's guided threshold or lactate test if offered. Training in the right zones is the difference between building fitness and just getting tired.

4

Week 4: Test race-day setup

Do a long run with your exact race-day configuration: the data screens you'll use, the alerts you'll set, the music setup. Discover problems on a training run, not at mile 18.

Myths Beginners Believe About Marathon Watches

❌ Myth: "I need the most expensive watch to finish"

Fact: A $149 Forerunner 55 has everything a first marathon requires. The extra $450 for a flagship buys maps, titanium, and metrics you won't use until marathon #3.

❌ Myth: "My phone's GPS is good enough"

Fact: Phones die. A 5-hour marathon with GPS, music, and screen-on will kill most phones — and a dead phone means no tracking, no emergency calls, no finish-line photo.

❌ Myth: "More data is always better"

Fact: Beginners drown in metrics. Distance, pace, heart rate, and a training plan beat twenty advanced charts you'll never open. Master the basics first.

❌ Myth: "I should buy the watch right before the race"

Fact: Buy it when training starts. The coaching features need weeks of your baseline data to give useful guidance — a watch bought on race week is just an expensive stopwatch.

1. Garmin Forerunner 165 — Best Overall

🏆 Our pick: If you buy one watch for your first marathon and stop reading here, make it this one. The Forerunner 165 is the most-recommended beginner running watch of 2026 for a simple reason: it gives you a gorgeous AMOLED display, Garmin's genuinely useful coaching ecosystem, and ~19 hours of GPS for $250.

The 165 fixed the one thing that held back every budget Garmin before it — the washed-out display. The 1.2-inch AMOLED touchscreen is bright, sharp, and makes mid-run stats actually readable in sunlight. Underneath, you get the features that matter for marathon training: Daily Suggested Workouts that adapt to your recovery, free Garmin Coach 5K-to-marathon plans, PacePro pacing strategies, VO2 max estimates, and Body Battery energy tracking.

What you don't get: the Training Readiness score, running power, and triathlon mode reserved for the 265 and up. For a first marathon, you won't miss them. The single-band GPS (no multi-band) is the one spec-sheet compromise, but in independent testing it tracks road running accurately — multi-band matters more for downtown skyscrapers and dense trail switchbacks than for marathon courses.

At 39 grams it's light enough to forget, and the 11-day smartwatch battery means you're charging roughly once a week in normal training — not every night like an Apple Watch.

Training with it: Set up Garmin Coach on day one with your marathon date. The Daily Suggested Workouts will build your week around the plan — easy runs genuinely easy (the watch buzzes if your heart rate drifts too high), workouts with clear interval guidance on screen. After 4–6 weeks, the race predictor gives you a surprisingly honest finish-time estimate to calibrate your goal.

Skip it if: you're an iPhone user who wants cellular calls and full smartwatch features on your wrist — that's the SE 3. Or if you know you'll obsess over training metrics — the 265's Readiness score will call to you.

2. Apple Watch SE 3 — Best for iPhone Users

📱 Our pick for iPhone users: The SE 3 is the cheapest ticket into the Apple Watch world — notifications, safety features, Apple Pay, and solid run tracking. But read the battery asterisk below before race day.

Let's correct something first: the SE 3 launched in September 2025, not 2026 — it runs the S10 chip (same as the 2025 Series 11), while the 2026 Series 12 moved to the S11 with its new Health Sensing System. That matters because the SE 3 lacks ECG, blood oxygen sensing, sleep apnea detection, and the always-on background heart-rate tracking of the Series 12.

For marathon training, what counts is this: GPS, wrist heart rate with alerts, an always-on OLED display, and 18 hours of rated battery (32 hours in Low Power Mode). The Workout app tracks runs well, and third-party apps like Nike Run Club and Strava fill any gaps. At 26 grams (40mm), it's the lightest watch on this list.

The honest asterisk: Apple doesn't publish a separate workout-mode GPS battery figure for the SE 3. The 18-hour rating covers mixed use. A 4-hour marathon is almost certainly fine; a 6-hour first marathon with music streaming and cellular is the scenario where we'd want more margin. If your expected finish is over 5:30, the Garmin and COROS options on this list give you far more headroom for the same money.

Training with it: The Workout app covers the basics well — custom intervals, pace alerts, heart-rate zones. Pair it with Nike Run Club or Strava for guided runs and social accountability. Enable Low Power Mode for long runs to stretch battery, and download offline music/playlists the night before. Pro tip: start the race with a full charge and cellular off (unless you need safety features) — that's the biggest battery lever you have.

Skip it if: you're on Android (it won't pair), or your expected finish is 5:30+ and you don't want to think about battery. The Forerunner 165 gives you triple the GPS headroom for the same price.

How to Choose: What Actually Matters for Your First Marathon

1. GPS battery: the 2× rule

Your watch must outlast your race comfortably. A 5-hour finisher needs ~10 hours of rated GPS once you add the warmup jog, the walk to the start corral, and the fact that batteries degrade. Every dedicated running watch here (19–41h) clears that bar with room to spare. The Apple Watch SE 3 is the only question mark — fine for most, tight if you're targeting 5:30+.

2. Wrist heart rate: non-negotiable

Every watch on this list has it. Marathon training lives in heart-rate zones — easy runs easy, workouts hard — and you can't do that with pace alone on hilly courses or hot days. Don't buy any watch without it in 2026.

3. A training plan built in

Garmin Coach (free on all four Garmins here) and COROS training plans turn the watch from a tracker into a coach. For a first marathon, this feature is worth more than any sensor upgrade.

4. What you can skip

Multi-band GPS (nice, not necessary for road marathons), offline maps, ECG, blood oxygen, titanium. The $150–$250 tier covers 100% of a beginner's needs. Spend the savings on shoes.

5. iPhone vs Android changes the answer

Only Apple Watches pair fully with iPhones — Garmins and COROS work with both but can't answer calls or reply to texts on iOS the way an Apple Watch can. If you're deep in Apple's ecosystem and want one device for everything, the SE 3's compromises may be worth it. If running comes first, Garmin/COROS win.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best running watch for a first marathon?

The Garmin Forerunner 165 ($250) is the most-recommended pick for 2026: AMOLED display, ~19 hours of GPS, and free Garmin Coach marathon plans. If battery matters most, the COROS PACE 4 ($249) offers 41 hours of GPS at the same price. On the tightest budget, the Forerunner 55 (often $149 on sale) is the classic purist pick.

Do I need GPS for marathon training?

Yes — GPS is the single most important feature. Marathon plans are built on distance and pace (long runs of 18–20 miles, tempo workouts at goal pace), and you can't follow them accurately without GPS. Every watch on this list has it.

How much GPS battery do I need for a marathon?

Use the 2× rule: your watch's rated GPS battery should be roughly double your expected finish time. A 4-hour finisher needs ~8 hours; a 6-hour finisher needs ~12. This covers your warmup, the walk to the start, and battery degradation. All the dedicated running watches here (19–41h) clear this easily.

Can I train for a marathon with an Apple Watch SE 3?

Yes, with one caveat. The SE 3 tracks runs well and has all the smartwatch conveniences, but Apple doesn't publish a workout-mode GPS battery figure — the 18-hour rating is for mixed use. For finish times under ~5 hours it's fine; beyond 5:30, a Garmin or COROS gives you far more race-day margin for the same money.

Is the COROS PACE 4 better than the Garmin Forerunner 165?

It depends on your priorities. The PACE 4 wins on battery (41h vs 19h GPS) and has dual-band GPS at the same $249 price. The Forerunner 165 wins on ecosystem — a bigger app selection, Garmin Pay, Spotify offline (Music edition), and more third-party integrations. For pure running value, the PACE 4; for the fuller smartwatch-plus-running package, the 165.

Do I need a heart-rate chest strap too?

Not to start. Every watch here has wrist-based heart rate that's accurate enough for zone-based beginner training. A chest strap (like the Garmin HRM series) is more precise for intervals and racing — consider one for your second marathon, not your first.

Should I buy the Forerunner 265 instead of the 165?

Only if you'll use the extra analytics. The 265 adds Training Readiness, HRV Status, running power, and multi-band GPS for $200 more. If you love training data and plan to train with structure for years, it's worth it. If you just want to follow a plan and finish your first marathon, the 165 does everything you need.

When should I buy my marathon watch?

At the start of your training block — ideally 16–20 weeks before race day. You want months of baseline data (resting heart rate, VO2 max estimates, training load) so the watch's coaching features actually know you by race day. Don't buy it the week before the marathon.
