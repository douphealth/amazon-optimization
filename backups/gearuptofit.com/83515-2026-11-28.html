❓ Google Pixel Watch 4: Frequently Asked Questions (2026)

Q: Do I need an active phone plan for the Satellite SOS to work?

A: No. This is a common misconception. The Satellite SOS feature requires the LTE hardware model of the Pixel Watch 4 but does not require an active cellular subscription with T-Mobile, Verizon, or AT&T. The watch communicates directly with the Iridium satellite network. You only pay if you trigger a rescue through Garmin Response (typically covered by adventure insurance).

Q: How accurate is the heart rate sensor compared to a Polar H10 or Garmin HRM-Pro Plus?

A: In my controlled tests against the Polar H10 chest strap (industry gold standard), the 4th-gen optical sensor performed admirably:

  • Steady-State Cardio (Zone 2-3): Within 2-3 BPM, 97% of the time.
  • High-Intensity Intervals (HIIT Workouts): Within 5-7 BPM during rapid spikes. This is typical for wrist-based optical sensors.
  • Strength Training: Less reliable due to wrist flexions; consider a chest strap for weightlifting accuracy.
Q: Is it worth upgrading from the Pixel Watch 3 in 2026?

A: The upgrade decision hinges on one feature: Satellite SOS.

  • YES, Upgrade If: You hike, trail run, ski, or adventure beyond reliable cell coverage. The SOS feature, brighter display, and dual-frequency GPS are worth it.
  • NO, Stick with Pixel Watch 3 If: You’re a city-dweller with consistent T-Mobile/Verizon coverage and are happy with your current battery life. The performance gains, while nice, aren’t essential.

With Pixel Watch 3 prices now around $299, it remains a fantastic value.

Q: Can I use it for swimming and diving?

A: Yes for swimming, no for diving. The 5ATM (50-meter) water resistance rating means it’s safe for pool and open-water swimming, showering, and snorkeling. It is not rated for high-velocity water sports (water skiing) or scuba diving. For that, you’d need a dive computer like the Garmin Descent Mk3 or Apple Watch Ultra 3.
