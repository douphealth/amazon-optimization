A 15-minute full body stretch should cover hips, hamstrings, spine, shoulders, and calves

Quick answer: A strong 15-minute full body stretch uses short, controlled holds across the major tight areas: neck and shoulders, thoracic spine, hips, hamstrings, quads, calves, and glutes. Move slowly, breathe through each position, and keep every stretch below sharp pain.

Best timeAfter training, after sitting, or on a light recovery day.
Hold lengthUse 30–45 seconds per stretch or side.
IntensityAim for mild-to-moderate tension, not pain.

Step-by-step action plan

  1. Start with slow breathing and gentle neck/shoulder movement.
  2. Use dynamic mobility first if you feel cold or stiff.
  3. Hold each major stretch steadily without bouncing.
  4. Switch sides evenly for hips, hamstrings, quads, and calves.
  5. Finish with nasal breathing to leave the routine feeling restorative.

FAQ

Is 15 minutes of stretching enough?
Yes for daily mobility maintenance and recovery. It is not a complete strength or flexibility program, but consistency matters more than occasional long sessions.

Should I stretch before or after a workout?
Use dynamic mobility before training and longer static holds after training or during recovery sessions.
