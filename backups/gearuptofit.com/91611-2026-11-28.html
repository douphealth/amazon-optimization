Eighteen versions in, the Brooks Ghost is the Toyota Camry of running shoes: nobody brags about buying one, yet it keeps selling because it starts every morning and gets you where you need to go. The Ghost 18 (released April 2026, $150) doesn’t reinvent the formula — Brooks kept the DNA LOFT v3 midsole and the 10 mm drop from the Ghost 17, and spent the update budget where the 17 was weakest: the upper. New triple-jacquard mesh, a softer flat-knit tongue, a revised sockliner, and a slightly roomier toe box.

If you already own the Ghost 17 and the fit works, the 18 is a side-grade, not an upgrade — the honest decision comes down to price and stock. But if you’re buying a Ghost for the first time, or you’re coming from an older 12 mm-drop model, this is the most comfortable version of the line in years. What’s new vs the Ghost 17

The short version: Brooks changed the part you touch, not the part you run on. The midsole — nitrogen-infused DNA LOFT v3, 10 mm drop — carries over from the Ghost 17 untouched. Reviewers who tested both (including Run to the Finish and Runners Lab) report essentially the same ride. The updates are all in comfort and fit:

  • Triple-jacquard engineered air mesh upper (double on the 17) — softer, more breathable, slightly roomier through the toe box. Marathon Sports specifically noted the 17’s upper felt stiff and overly structured; this is the direct fix.
  • Flat-knit pillow tongue — thicker, stretchier, wraps the instep instead of pressing on it; stays put better than the narrower 17 tongue.
  • New Ortholite X-60 sockliner — softer step-in feel out of the box.
  • Lower, softer heel collar — Brooks confirmed the collar doesn’t sit as high on the Achilles, addressing a complaint some runners had with the 17.
  • More reflective details on the heel for low-light visibility.
  • Revised foam formula, slightly less firm than the 17 (per Runners Lab) — a subtle tuning, not a new foam.

Context that matters: the big change in this line already happened with the Ghost 16 → 17, when Brooks moved from the long-standing 12 mm drop to 10 mm. The 18 is a consolidation year. If a deal puts a 17 in your cart at $110–120 in your size, take the money.

🔧 You know what changed on the outside — now let’s look at what’s happening inside the midsole, and why Brooks deliberately left the ride alone.

Technology deep-dive

DNA LOFT v3 — what nitrogen infusion actually does

DNA LOFT v3 is Brooks’ nitrogen-infused EVA foam. The nitrogen gas is injected into the foam cells during manufacturing, which makes the material lighter and softer than standard EVA without going full super-foam. Expect a firm-cushioned, planted ride — Marathon Sports describes it as a “relatively firm, stable feel for smooth and secure landings.” This is deliberate: the Ghost is aimed at runners who want to feel the ground and land predictably, not at runners who want the trampoline sensation of PEBA-based foams. The foam also has a wide base, which gives the shoe inherent stability even though it’s a neutral trainer with no guidance rails.

The upper: where version 18 earns its money

This is the most meaningful part of the update. The triple-jacquard mesh is softer and more breathable than the 17’s double-jacquard, with a bit more give in the toe box. The flat-knit tongue is genuinely new — it’s stretchier and more padded, and it wraps the top of the foot rather than creating a pressure line across the instep. Extra heel padding and a lowered collar address the Achilles-rub complaint some runners had with the 17. Fit runs true to size, and the toe box has a touch more stretch than the 17 without changing the nominal sizing.

RoadTack outsole

Full-coverage rubber made with recycled silica — the emphasis is durability over weight savings. Reviewers consistently call Ghost outsoles long-lived, which is part of why the shoe is a high-mileage favorite. Wet-road traction is adequate for a road trainer (standard rubber, no lug pattern), but it’s not a rain shoe — that’s what the $180 GTX version is for.

Step-in comfort: Ortholite X-60

Brooks swapped the sockliner to Ortholite X-60. This mostly affects first-impression comfort — the “ahh” moment in the store — rather than the running ride, which is dictated by the midsole underneath. Still, for walkers and all-day wearers (nurses, teachers, retail workers — a huge chunk of Ghost buyers), it matters.

Fit and sizing: Ghost-specific notes

The Ghost line is famously consistent — reviewers report the 18 fits true to size in length, matching other Brooks models. A few specifics worth knowing before you order:

  • Toe box: the new triple-jacquard mesh has a touch more stretch than the 17, so the toe box feels slightly roomier without changing the official size. Runners who found the 17 snug across the forefoot get relief here; if the 17 fit perfectly, don’t size down — the difference is in give, not length.
  • Heel: extra padding plus the lowered collar means a softer wrap around the Achilles. Brooks explicitly addressed the 17’s higher, stiffer collar. Heel slip hasn’t been reported as an issue in early reviews, and the refined heel counter improves lockdown.
  • Widths are the real story: most daily trainers stop at wide. The Ghost 18’s extra-wide (4E) men’s option is rare in this category and a legitimate reason to pick it over the Clifton 11 or Pegasus 42 if standard widths pinch you.
  • Orthotics: the removable Ortholite X-60 sockliner comes out cleanly, leaving usable volume for custom insoles — relevant for the walker/all-day-wear crowd this shoe serves.

Durability and replacement timing

Full-coverage RoadTack rubber is the durability story: there’s no exposed foam on the outsole to chew through, unlike some lighter trainers. Independent reviewers (Runners Lab, Run to the Finish) rate Ghost durability as very good, consistent with the line’s reputation for surviving full training cycles. Standard guidance still applies — most neutral daily trainers of this build last roughly 300–500 miles depending on runner weight, gait, and surface. Watch for a dead-feeling, uneven midsole rather than waiting for the tread to go bald; the DNA LOFT v3 foam will feel flat and less protective before the rubber is gone.

The GTX version: worth $30 more?

The Ghost 18 GTX ($180) adds a GORE-TEX Invisible Fit waterproof membrane bonded directly to the upper, plus an outsole tuned for wet traction. REI lists the women’s GTX at 10 oz and the men’s at 11.2 oz per shoe (half-pair weights) — noticeably heavier than the standard version, and waterproof shoes always run warmer. Buy the GTX only if you routinely run in rain; for dry climates and treadmill use, the standard version breathes better and costs less.

FAQ

What is the Brooks Ghost 18 best for?
Daily road training: easy runs, long aerobic miles, recovery days, treadmill sessions, and walking. It’s a neutral trainer with moderate cushioning — built for comfort and durability across everyday mileage, not for racing or speed work.
What’s the difference between the Ghost 18 and the Ghost 17?
The midsole is the same nitrogen-infused DNA LOFT v3 with the same 10 mm drop, so the ride is nearly identical. The 18 upgrades the upper: triple-jacquard mesh, a softer flat-knit tongue, a new Ortholite X-60 sockliner, a lower heel collar, and more reflective details. If the 17 fits you well, the 18 is a side-grade — buy whichever is cheaper.
Does the Ghost 18 have a 10 mm or 12 mm drop?
10 mm. Brooks moved the Ghost from 12 mm to 10 mm with the Ghost 17, and the 18 keeps the 10 mm drop. If you’re coming from a Ghost 16 or older, expect a slightly less heel-forward feel.
How much does the Brooks Ghost 18 weigh?
Brooks lists 10.2 oz (289 g) for men’s and 9.2 oz (260 g) for women’s. Independent scale checks land within about half an ounce of those figures depending on size.
Is the Ghost 18 good for walking and all-day wear?
Yes — this is one of its strengths. The cushioned heel, stable flat geometry, durable RoadTack outsole, and wide/extra-wide options make it popular with walkers and people who stand all day. The GORE-TEX version is worth considering if you’re out in wet weather.
Is the Ghost 18 good for beginners?
Yes. It’s one of the most commonly recommended first running shoes: neutral, forgiving, true to size, durable, and available in four widths. The predictable ride means fewer surprises while your form is still developing.
Does the Ghost 18 come in wide sizes?
Yes — unusually wide range: men’s narrow (B), standard (D), wide (2E), and extra-wide (4E); women’s narrow (A), standard (B), and wide (D). Few daily trainers offer an extra-wide option.
Is the Ghost 18 good for speed work or racing?
Not its strength. There’s no plate, no aggressive rocker, and the DNA LOFT v3 foam prioritizes comfort over energy return. It handles strides and relaxed tempo fine, but for real speed work you’d pair it with a tempo shoe.
How much does the Ghost 18 cost, and is the GTX version worth it?
The standard Ghost 18 is $150. The waterproof GORE-TEX (GTX) version is $180. The GTX is worth it if you regularly run in rain; otherwise the standard version breathes better.
Should I buy the Ghost 17 or the Ghost 18?
The ride is the same midsole, so decide on price and fit: buy the 18 if you want the softer tongue, roomier toe box, and lower heel collar; buy the 17 if you can find your size significantly cheaper. Don’t pay a premium for the 18 expecting a different run.
