What actually makes a watch a triathlon watch

Not every GPS watch can race a triathlon. Three features separate true multisport watches from running watches with a swim mode:

1
Multisport mode with transitions
One button press moves you from swim to bike to run, recording transition times. Without this, you’re juggling three separate activities on race day. The Forerunner 570, Ultra 4, and Enduro 4 all have it; the Forerunner 165 covers the sports individually via 25+ modes.
2
Open-water swim GPS that works
Pool swimming is easy — open water is where cheap watches fall apart. Multi-band GPS (Forerunner 570, Ultra 4’s dual-frequency) holds signal when your wrist is underwater half the stroke. This is the single biggest reason to avoid budget running watches for triathlon.
3
Battery for YOUR distance, plus margin
A sprint tri takes 1–2 hours; an Ironman can take 17. Your watch needs full-GPS battery comfortably beyond your expected finish time — GPS, heart rate, and music all drain faster than spec sheets suggest. See the race-distance guide below.

5 mistakes triathletes make buying a watch

✗

Buying for the longest race you’ll never do.

If you race sprints, you don’t need 320-hour battery. Match the watch to the races on your actual calendar.
✗

Ignoring open-water swim GPS.

Pool swim tracking is easy. Open water is where single-band GPS falls apart — if you race in lakes or ocean, multi-band/dual-frequency is worth paying for.
✗

Trusting spec-sheet battery for race day.

Music, navigation alerts, and cold water all drain faster than the headline number. Always test on a long brick workout first.
✗

Choosing ecosystem over race needs.

Love your iPhone? Great — but if you’re racing Ironman, be honest about whether 18-hour GPS with battery-saver modes is enough, or whether Garmin’s endurance is the safer bet.
✗

Skipping the transition practice.

Multisport mode is only useful if your fingers know the button sequence under race stress. Practice transitions with the watch at least twice before race day.

Triathlon watch FAQ

Do I need a triathlon-specific watch, or will a running watch work?
A running watch works for training, but on race day you want multisport mode with automatic transitions — otherwise you’re manually starting/stopping three activities while trying to race. Every watch on this list except the budget Forerunner 165 has a dedicated multisport mode.
How much GPS battery do I really need for an Ironman?
Take your expected finish time and double it — GPS, heart rate, and music drain faster than spec sheets suggest, and cold/wet conditions drain faster still. For most athletes (10–14 hour finish), the Forerunner 570’s 18 hours or Ultra 4’s extended 45-hour mode is enough with margin. If you want zero anxiety, the Enduro 4’s 320-hour solar battery ends the conversation.
Apple Watch Ultra 4 vs Garmin for triathlon — which is better?
The Ultra 4 is the better smartwatch — apps, calls, dive computer, satellite SOS — with genuinely elite heart-rate accuracy (DC Rainmaker: “definitely the best Apple has ever made”). Garmin wins on GPS battery life, maps/navigation (“the single biggest gap” per DC Rainmaker), and training ecosystem depth. iPhone athletes racing up to 70.3: Ultra 4. Long-course or data-driven athletes: Garmin.
Can I use a triathlon watch as a daily smartwatch?
Yes — and this is where the AMOLED models (Forerunner 570, Ultra 4, Forerunner 165) pull ahead of the MIP-display Enduro 4. The 570 and Ultra 4 look and feel like modern smartwatches; the Enduro 4 looks like the tool it is.
What’s the cheapest watch that can actually race a triathlon?
The Garmin Forerunner 165 at $249.99 — 25+ sport modes, 19-hour GPS, Garmin Coach. It lacks multi-band GPS and a one-button multisport transition mode, so you’ll manage transitions manually and swim GPS will be less precise in open water. For sprint distance, that’s a completely reasonable trade at this price.
What is multisport mode on a triathlon watch?
Multisport mode lets you record swim, bike, and run as one continuous activity with automatic transition timing. You press one button (usually the lap button) when you exit the water and again when you leave transition — the watch logs each leg plus your T1/T2 times separately. Without it, you’d have to manually stop and start three separate activities mid-race.
Is the Apple Watch Ultra 4 water resistant enough for triathlon swimming?
Yes. The Ultra 4 is rated WR100 (100m water resistance) with EN13319 dive certification, and it has a dedicated open-water swim mode plus automatic multisport transitions. It’s the first Apple Watch genuinely suitable for triathlon — earlier models lacked the battery life, not the water resistance.
How accurate is wrist heart rate during a triathlon?
It depends on the sensor and the sport. DC Rainmaker’s testing found the Apple Watch Ultra 4’s sensor “definitely the best Apple has ever made” — nearly matching a chest strap on a 22-mile run. Garmin’s Elevate v5 (Forerunner 570) is also strong for running and cycling. But no wrist sensor matches a chest strap in the swim leg — water disrupts optical readings. For race-critical HR data, pair any of these watches with a chest strap.
Forerunner 570 vs 970 for triathlon — is the 970 worth $200 more?
The Forerunner 970 ($749.99) adds onboard maps, an LED flashlight, and ECG over the 570. If you navigate unfamiliar courses or train on trails, maps alone may justify it. For most triathletes racing marked courses, the 570’s $549.99 price and identical triathlon coaching make it the better value — that’s $200 toward race entries.
Can a triathlon watch track my transitions?
Yes — that’s exactly what multisport mode is for. When you press the lap/transition button, the watch stops the current leg’s timer and starts a transition timer, so your final file shows swim time, T1, bike time, T2, and run time as separate splits. All five watches in this guide support transition tracking except the budget Forerunner 165, which requires manual activity switching.

Most triathletes should buy the Garmin Forerunner 570.
At $549.99 it’s the only watch here that combines AMOLED, multi-band GPS, true triathlon coaching, and 18-hour battery without flagship pricing. iPhone loyalists: the Ultra 4.
