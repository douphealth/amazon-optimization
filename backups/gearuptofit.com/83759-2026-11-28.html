Price, Models, and Where It Sits in the Vomero Lineup

The Nike Vomero Plus launched in August 2025 at a retail price of $180 in the US (around £160 in the UK). To understand the Plus, you need to understand what Nike did to its running catalog: the brand reorganized everything into three franchises — Pegasus (responsive cushioning), Vomero (maximum cushioning), and Structure (supportive cushioning). Within the Vomero franchise there are now three tiers:

  • Vomero 18 (the Icon, $155) — the entry-level max-cushion trainer. Dual-density midsole: ZoomX on top, ReactX underneath. Heavier (11.5 oz men’s), but cheaper and a touch more structured.

Simple rule of thumb: the higher you climb, the taller and more premium the cushioning gets — and the more you pay. The Plus is the sweet spot for most runners: you get the full ZoomX experience for $40 less than the Premium.

Technology Deep-Dive: Why Full-Length ZoomX Changes Everything

The single most important thing to understand about the Vomero Plus is the foam — not the stack height, not the name, the foam. Here’s what full-length ZoomX actually means and why reviewers keep coming back to it.

What ZoomX is

ZoomX is Nike’s premium midsole foam, originally developed for its elite marathon racing shoes. Chemically, it’s a PEBAX-based foam (a lightweight, highly resilient polymer) that returns more energy per stride than standard EVA foams. Nike’s own product copy calls it its most responsive foam — the same foam you’ll find in race-day shoes costing $250+. Putting it in an $180 daily trainer is the whole point of the Plus.

Full-length ZoomX vs. the Vomero 18’s dual-density setup

This is the difference that defines the two shoes, so let’s be precise — because it’s easy to get wrong. (A previous version of this page described the Plus as having a ZoomX-plus-React construction; that is the Vomero 18’s setup, not the Plus’s. The correction matters, because the ride difference comes entirely from this choice.)

  • Vomero 18: a ZoomX layer on top of a ReactX base. ReactX is firmer and more durable, so the 18 feels more structured and muted — plush, but calm.
  • Vomero Plus: ZoomX from top to bottom. Same premium foam throughout, which is why the ride is softer at step-in and springier at toe-off.

Why doesn’t every shoe just use full ZoomX? Two reasons: cost (ZoomX is expensive to produce) and stability. Very soft, very tall foam can feel wobbly, especially for heavier runners or uneven strides. Nike’s answer in the Plus is geometry rather than firmer foam: a wide midsole base with raised sidewalls that cradle the foot, plus a beveled heel and a late-stage forefoot rocker that smooth out the transition from landing to toe-off. The result, by most independent reviewer accounts, is a tall shoe that doesn’t feel tippy — “plush but controlled” is the consensus description.

No plate, no Air — and that’s deliberate

The Vomero Plus contains no carbon plate and no Air Zoom units. That isn’t a missing feature; it’s a design philosophy. Plates add stiffness and snap, but they also add cost and make a shoe feel less natural at easy paces — most runners don’t want a stiff lever underfoot for a slow 45-minute jog. The Plus is built for the other 80% of your training: the miles where comfort and joint protection matter more than propulsion.

The $220 Vomero Premium exists for anyone who wants the same ZoomX cushioning with added Air Zoom snap and an even taller stack. Think of the Plus as the pure-foam option and the Premium as the max-everything option.

Upper, outsole, and the details

The upper is a soft engineered mesh designed for breathability — a welcome trait, because tall cushioned shoes can run warm. Multiple reviewers have noted the Plus fits true to size with a slightly tapered toe box, and Nike offers wide versions, which is genuinely useful in the max-cushion category where uppers tend to run snug over high-volume feet.

The outsole uses high-abrasion rubber with full-contact coverage — no big exposed foam windows. That’s a meaningful durability choice: exposed foam saves weight but wears fast on asphalt, and daily trainers live on asphalt. The continuous outsole also gives the shoe its sure-footed, full-ground-contact feel through the whole stride.

Max-Cushion Context: What 45 mm of Foam Actually Does for You

“Max cushion” is a marketing term, so let’s translate it into biomechanics. Every footstrike sends an impact force up through your ankles, knees, and hips. Cushioning foam compresses and spreads that force over a longer time, reducing the peak load. A 45 mm heel stack — among the tallest legal for road running (World Athletics caps road shoes at 40 mm, but that limit applies to competition, not training) — gives the foam an enormous amount of material to work with.

The honest trade-offs of max cushioning:

  • Pro: Less peak impact per step. For high-mileage runners, heavier runners, and anyone coming back to running, that reduction in pounding is the entire selling point.
  • Pro: Legs feel fresher on long slow runs. Tall, soft stacks are why max-cushion shoes dominate the “first marathon” recommendation lists.
  • Con: More weight and less ground feel. A 10.3 oz shoe will never feel nimble next to an 8 oz tempo trainer.
  • Con: Energy return is real but not magic. ZoomX is bouncy, but no foam returns 100% of the energy you put in — the laws of physics don’t do marketing.
  • Con: Tall stacks can feel unstable for some runners, especially on cambered roads or trails. The Plus mitigates this with its wide base, but it’s worth a try-on if you’re stability-sensitive.

Max-cushion shoes also aren’t injury-prevention devices — more foam doesn’t guarantee fewer injuries, and anyone with persistent pain should see a clinician rather than buy a taller shoe. What max cushioning does reliably is make running feel easier, which keeps people consistent. And consistency is where fitness comes from.

Nike Vomero Plus FAQ

Is the Nike Vomero Plus good for running?

Yes — for easy miles, long slow runs, and recovery days. The Vomero Plus is a max-cushion neutral daily trainer with a full-length ZoomX midsole, so it’s built for comfort over distance. It is not a speed or racing shoe: there’s no carbon plate, and at 10.3 oz (men’s) it’s heavier than tempo trainers.

Is the Vomero Plus better than the Vomero 18?

For most runners, yes — with one caveat. The Plus ($180) uses a full-length ZoomX midsole and weighs 10.3 oz (men’s), while the Vomero 18 ($155) uses a dual-density ZoomX-over-ReactX setup and weighs 11.5 oz. The Plus is lighter and more energetic. The 18’s advantages are its lower price and a slightly firmer, more structured ride that some heavier runners prefer.

What’s the difference between the Nike Vomero Plus and the Nike Invincible 3?

The Vomero Plus is effectively the Invincible line’s successor. Both use a full-length ZoomX midsole, but the Plus stacks taller (45/35 mm vs. roughly 40/31 mm), weighs about an ounce less (men’s), and adds a wider platform with raised midsole sidewalls for better stability. The Invincible line has been discontinued, so the Plus is Nike’s current answer for Invincible fans.

Does the Nike Vomero Plus have a carbon plate?

No. The Vomero Plus has no carbon plate. It’s a pure-foam daily trainer — the propulsion comes from the geometry (beveled heel, forefoot rocker) and the energy return of the ZoomX foam, not from a stiff plate. If you want a plated Nike trainer, look at the racing line instead.

Does the Nike Vomero Plus have Air Zoom units?

No. The Plus relies entirely on its full-length ZoomX foam midsole. Air Zoom units appear in the step-up model, the $220 Vomero Premium, which pairs ZoomX with two Air Zoom units (heel and forefoot) and a taller 55/45 mm stack.

How much does the Nike Vomero Plus cost?

The retail price is $180 in the US (about £160 in the UK). It sits between the $155 Vomero 18 and the $220 Vomero Premium in Nike’s Vomero lineup. Popular colorways are frequently discounted by third-party sellers on Amazon.

How much does the Nike Vomero Plus weigh?

Nike lists 10.3 oz (292 g) for a men’s US 10 and 8.5 oz (242 g) for a women’s US 8. Independent lab measurements of a men’s US 9 have come in around 9.9 oz — normal variation by size and scale. Either way, it’s over an ounce lighter than the Vomero 18.

Is the Nike Vomero Plus good for beginners or a first marathon?

It’s one of the strongest Nike options for both. Beginners benefit from the impact protection and the forgiving, stable-feeling ride, and first-time marathoners whose goal is finishing comfortably will appreciate the tall ZoomX stack on long runs. Just make sure to break in any new shoe with a few shorter runs before race day.

Does the Nike Vomero Plus fit true to size? Is there a wide version?

Yes, it fits true to size — order your normal running shoe size. The toe box is slightly tapered, so runners with wide feet should consider the wide version, which Nike offers for this model.

Is the Nike Vomero Plus good for walking or standing all day?

On paper, yes: max cushioning, a full-contact rubber outsole, and a breathable mesh upper are exactly what you want for all-day wear. Many max-cushion running shoes double as excellent walking shoes. The 10 mm drop suits heel strikers, which most walkers are.

How does the Vomero Plus compare to the Hoka Clifton 10?

Both are neutral max-comfort daily trainers, but they feel different. The Vomero Plus ($180, 45/35 mm, 10 mm drop, ZoomX foam) rides bouncier and more energetic; the Clifton 10 ($150, 42/34 mm, 8 mm drop, EVA foam) rides softer, smoother, and more muted, and it’s about half an ounce lighter. Choose the Plus for energy, the Clifton for the gentlest ride and the lower price.

Is the Nike Vomero Plus waterproof?

No. The engineered mesh upper is designed for breathability, not water resistance. Like most road running shoes, it will soak through in heavy rain. If you need wet-weather protection, look for a Gore-Tex or waterproof variant of a daily trainer instead.
