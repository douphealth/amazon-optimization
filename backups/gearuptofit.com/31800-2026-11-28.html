What Is Total Daily Energy Expenditure?

Your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) is like your body’s personal calorie furnace! It’s the total number of calories you burn each day, considering your age, weight, height, and activity level. Knowing your TDEE is key to weight loss or gain goals. Eat fewer calories than you burn to lose weight, and more to gain muscle!

TDEE isn’t just a straightforward calculation. It also accounts for weight gain over time, which can slow your metabolism. If you have more muscle mass, your TDEE is higher. That’s because muscles burn more energy than fat. When you aim to lose weight by changing your diet or activity level, your TDEE decreases. This prevents your body from burning too many calories too fast.

How Does My Metabolism Work?

Your metabolism is a process that converts the food you eat and oxygen into energy. It’s essential to your life and happens in every cell in your body.

Your metabolism involves every chemical reaction in your body. It also covers activities like breathing and heartbeat, where atoms don’t change. This process determines how your body uses energy from food. It controls whether this energy is quickly used, saved as fat, or turned into muscle. Additionally, metabolism involves how much heat your body creates during digestion. This heat helps convert food into fuel for daily activities.

Calculating Your BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate)

You can calculate your BMR by multiplying your weight in pounds by 10, and then adding the result to 6.25.

So if you weigh 190 pounds: 190 x 10 = 1,900 + 6.25 = 2,015 calories per day

Now that we know how many calories you burn at rest, let’s figure out how many calories you burn while exercising.

Calculating TEF (Thermic Effect of Food)

The thermic effect of food (TEF) is the amount of calories your body burns digesting food. It’s a small part of your total calorie burn, but it can be significant over time.

The TEF makes up 10% of your daily energy use, or TDEE. If you eat 2,000 calories daily, as recommended, you burn 200 calories just by digesting food. Eating high-quality foods is more motivating because proteins have a higher TEF than carbs!

TDEE Factors to Consider

TDEE depends on several factors, including your gender, age, height, weight, and body composition.

The most important factor is your RMR. This is the number of calories you burn at rest—when not exercising or doing anything else to expend energy. If you’re sedentary (meaning that you spend a lot of time sitting), then this number will be higher than if you move more and regularly exercise.

Another thing that affects TDEE is the thermic effect of food (TEF). When we eat food—especially protein—our bodies use some energy digesting it into smaller molecules that can pass through our cells’ walls as nutrients.

Your TDEE is your BMR multiplied by your activity factor.

  • An activity factor increases the calories you burn based on your BMR. This is because it considers your daily activity level.
