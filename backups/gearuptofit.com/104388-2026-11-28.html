Running Features: What You Actually Get

The headline story of the Forerunner 165 is that Garmin didn’t gut the training engine to hit $249.99. Here’s what the spec sheet promises — and what each feature means in practice:

Garmin Coach — free adaptive training plans for 5K, 10K, and half marathon, built around your goal race date and current fitness. This is the same coaching platform as the flagship models; the watch adjusts workouts as your fitness changes.

Daily suggested workouts — every morning the watch proposes a run tuned to your recent training load, recovery time, and sleep. It’s the closest thing to a coach-in-your-pocket at this price.

Race adaptive training — set a goal race and the watch builds and adapts a training plan around it, pulling in your training history and recovery data.

VO2 max estimate, training effect & training load — the holy trinity of Garmin training metrics. VO2 max estimates your aerobic fitness, training effect scores each workout’s aerobic/anaerobic impact, and training load tracks whether your week is productive, maintaining, or overreaching.

Recovery time & race predictor — recovery time tells you how long to wait before the next hard effort; race predictor forecasts your 5K/10K/half/marathon times based on your current fitness.

What it does NOT have: the Training Readiness score (a single morning number blending sleep, recovery, HRV, and training history), Hill and Endurance scores, and cycling power meter support — those are reserved for the Forerunner 265 tier and above. If a single “am I ready to push today?” number matters to you, that’s the upsell Garmin is making.

The Single-Band GPS Reality Check

Let’s be honest about the one spec where the 165 shows its price. The Forerunner 165 uses single-band multi-GNSS (GPS + GLONASS + Galileo) — it listens to several satellite networks, but only on one frequency each. Multi-band (dual-frequency) GPS, found on the Forerunner 265, 570, and 970, listens on two frequencies simultaneously, which dramatically reduces signal bounce off buildings and tree canopy.

What this means on the road: under open sky — suburban roads, tracks, parks — single-band GNSS is broadly accurate and this is not a meaningful concern. The published reviews (Tom’s Guide, TechRadar) report solid everyday tracking. Where the limitation shows up is dense downtowns with tall buildings and heavy tree cover, where tracks can drift or cut corners more than a multi-band watch would.

If most of your miles happen in the city core or on forest trails and pinpoint route accuracy matters to you, that’s a legitimate reason to step up to the 265. For everyone else — track sessions, suburban loops, park runs — the 165’s GPS is fit for purpose. Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Garmin Forerunner 165 good for beginner runners?
On paper, it’s one of the strongest beginner picks in 2026. Garmin Coach builds free adaptive 5K, 10K, and half-marathon plans around your goal, daily suggested workouts tell you what to run each day, and the race predictor shows what times your current fitness supports. You get the coaching infrastructure of watches costing twice as much — the main things beginners miss versus the 265 are the Training Readiness score and multi-band GPS, neither of which is essential for someone building base mileage.
Should I buy the Forerunner 165 or the 165 Music?
The two models are identical except for music: both have the same 1.2-inch AMOLED display, the same sensors, the same training features, and the same 4GB of storage. The $299.99 Music version adds offline playlist sync from Spotify, Amazon Music, and Deezer so you can run phone-free with Bluetooth headphones. If you always run with your phone, save the $50 and buy the $249.99 base model.
Does the Forerunner 165 have multi-band GPS?
No. The Forerunner 165 uses single-band multi-GNSS — it connects to GPS, GLONASS, and Galileo satellites, but only on one frequency per system. Multi-band (dual-frequency) GPS is reserved for the Forerunner 265 tier and above. In practice this means accurate tracking under open sky, with potentially wobblier routes in dense downtown areas and heavy tree cover where signal bounce is worst.
What’s missing on the 165 compared to the Forerunner 265?
Five things, per Garmin’s spec sheets: (1) the Training Readiness score, (2) Hill and Endurance scores, (3) multi-band/dual-frequency GPS, (4) cycling power meter support, and (5) storage — 4GB on the 165 versus 8GB on the 265. The 265 also has a slightly larger 1.3-inch AMOLED display and costs $449, a full $200 more than the base 165.
How long does the Forerunner 165 battery really last?
Garmin rates it at up to 11 days in smartwatch mode, up to 20 days in battery-saver mode, up to 19 hours of GPS-only tracking, and up to 17 hours with all satellite systems enabled. These are Garmin’s estimates under default settings. With an hour of tracked running per day plus normal smartwatch use, most runners should expect roughly a week or more between charges — dramatically better than the 1–2 days of typical full smartwatches. Always-on display mode, music streaming, and all-day Pulse Ox will reduce these figures.
Can I swim with the Forerunner 165?
Yes. It’s rated 5 ATM for water resistance, and Garmin lists both pool swimming and open-water swimming among its 25+ activity profiles. That puts it on par with every other watch in this comparison for swim tracking.
Does the Forerunner 165 work with iPhone and Android?
Yes — the Forerunner 165 is compatible with both iOS and Android via the Garmin Connect app. Smart notifications, LiveTrack, and incident detection work on both platforms.
Does Garmin Pay work on the Forerunner 165?
Yes. The Forerunner 165 includes Garmin Pay with an NFC chip for contactless payments, so you can leave your wallet at home on runs. Notably, the cheaper Forerunner 55 does not offer Garmin Pay — it’s one of the clearest upgrades the 165 brings to the budget tier.
How much storage does the Forerunner 165 have?
4GB on both the base and Music versions. On the Music version, that storage holds offline playlists from Spotify, Amazon Music, and Deezer. It’s half the 8GB offered by the Forerunner 265, so heavy music users who also want lots of playlists should keep the limit in mind.
Forerunner 165 vs. Coros Pace 3 — which budget pick wins?
It depends on what you value. The Forerunner 165 ($249.99) wins on display — its 1.2-inch AMOLED screen is far nicer than the Pace 3’s MIP screen — and on ecosystem: Garmin Pay, the Garmin Coach training platform, and 25+ activity profiles. The Coros Pace 3 ($229) wins on battery (24 days / 38 hours GPS versus 11 days / 19 hours) and has dual-frequency GPS at a lower price. Pick the 165 for the screen and Garmin’s training features; pick the Pace 3 for maximum battery and GPS hardware per dollar.
