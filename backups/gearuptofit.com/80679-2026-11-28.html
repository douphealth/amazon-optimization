ASICS Novablast 4 specs: the accurate, fact-checked version

The Novablast 4 is a neutral road running shoe with high cushioning, an 8 mm drop, FF BLAST PLUS ECO midsole foam, an engineered woven upper, a tongue-wing fit system, and trampoline-inspired outsole geometry. It is designed for bounce and daily training, not motion-control stability, waterproofing or trail grip.

Important correction for SEO and trust: do not describe the Novablast 4 as a GEL-cushioning shoe. The cushioning story is FF BLAST PLUS ECO. Who should buy the ASICS Novablast 4?

Buy the Novablast 4 if you want one shoe to make ordinary mileage feel easier. Its best role is as a neutral daily trainer for easy runs, recovery runs, treadmill sessions, relaxed long runs, and steady aerobic mileage. Ride review: soft, bouncy and more controlled than the early Novablast reputation

The Novablast line became popular because it made daily training feel energetic instead of dull. The Novablast 4 keeps that identity, but it feels more mature than the original “wild trampoline” concept. The tall midsole gives you a soft landing. The geometric outsole helps the shoe rebound. The broader platform and sidewall shaping make it less unstable than a very narrow high-stack trainer.

Easy runs

This is the shoe’s home territory. It protects the legs, feels lively at relaxed pace, and works well when you want to keep the effort conversational.

Long runs

The cushioning-to-weight ratio makes it a strong long-run trainer for neutral runners. It is not as premium as Superblast, but it is far more affordable when discounted.

Tempo work

It can handle steady pace and moderate uptempo segments, but it is not a dedicated speed shoe. For hard intervals or racing, use something lighter and snappier.

Think of the Novablast 4 as a daily-mileage amplifier. It is not trying to be a carbon-plated race shoe. It is trying to make repetitive road miles more comfortable and more enjoyable. That is why it makes sense for aerobic base building, marathon training support runs, and runners who want one enjoyable neutral trainer.

Best training match: easy road runs, recovery runs, treadmill sessions, steady long runs, and moderate progression runs. For a faster rotation, keep Novablast 4 for volume and add a workout shoe separately.

Stability and pronation: stable for a neutral shoe, not a stability shoe

The Novablast 4 is more stable than its bouncy personality suggests, but it remains a neutral shoe. That distinction matters. Neutral stability means the platform feels reasonably controlled for runners with normal pronation. It does not mean the shoe has the kind of guidance system, medial support or structured stability that overpronators may need.

Good match

Neutral runners, mild underpronators, heel strikers who like a broad cushioned base, and runners who want a softer daily trainer without a mushy or collapsed feel.

Poor match

Moderate to severe overpronators, runners with recurring arch collapse, and anyone who has been advised to use stability footwear. Compare the ASICS Gel-Kayano 32 or our best overpronation shoes instead.

If you are on the border between neutral and stability, the Novablast 4 may work for shorter, easy miles. But for high weekly mileage, long runs on tired legs, or a history of shin, knee or arch pain tied to overpronation, stability should win over bounce.

Durability, outsole and grip: good for roads, limited for trails

The Novablast 4 outsole is made for road running. The AHAR rubber zones help protect high-wear areas, while exposed midsole sections keep weight down. This is the usual tradeoff for a high-cushion daily trainer: enough rubber where you need it, not so much that the shoe becomes heavy.

On dry pavement, concrete, track lanes and treadmill belts, the Novablast 4 makes sense. On wet painted lines, slick leaves, muddy shoulders or uneven gravel, slow down. The outsole is more daily-road than all-terrain. If you want a trail shoe, buy a trail shoe.

FAQ: ASICS Novablast 4

Is the ASICS Novablast 4 still worth buying in 2026?

Yes, if it is discounted. The Novablast 4 is still a strong neutral daily trainer for easy runs, long runs, treadmill mileage and steady road training. If it costs about the same as the Novablast 5, buy the newer Novablast 5 instead.

What type of runner is the Novablast 4 best for?

It is best for neutral runners who want a cushioned, bouncy road running shoe for daily mileage. It suits easy runs, recovery runs, long runs and moderate steady efforts better than racing or aggressive intervals.

Does the ASICS Novablast 4 have GEL cushioning?

No. The Novablast 4’s key cushioning technology is FF BLAST PLUS ECO foam. Official product descriptions focus on FF BLAST PLUS ECO, the engineered woven upper, AHAR outsole rubber and trampoline-inspired outsole design.

Is the ASICS Novablast 4 good for flat feet or overpronation?

It is not the best first choice for flat feet or overpronation. The Novablast 4 is a neutral shoe. Some mild overpronators may be fine in it, but runners who need clear guidance should compare ASICS Kayano, ASICS GT-2000 or other stability shoes.

Is the ASICS Novablast 4 true to size?

Most runners should start true to size. If you are between sizes, wear thick socks, use orthotics or need extra forefoot volume, consider trying a half-size up or checking wide-width alternatives.

Can you use the Novablast 4 for marathon training?

Yes, many runners can use it for marathon training mileage, especially easy runs, recovery runs, relaxed long runs and steady aerobic sessions. For race-pace workouts or the marathon itself, many runners will prefer a lighter performance trainer or racing shoe.

Is the ASICS Novablast 4 waterproof?

No. The upper is designed for stretch, ventilation and comfort, not waterproofing. Choose a weatherproof shoe if you regularly run in heavy rain, slush or cold puddles.

How long does the ASICS Novablast 4 last?

Most daily running shoes are replaced around 300–500 miles, depending on runner weight, gait, surfaces and wear pattern. Replace the Novablast 4 sooner if the outsole wears unevenly, the midsole feels flat, the heel collar breaks down, or new aches appear without a training explanation.

Is Novablast 4 better than Novablast 5?

The Novablast 4 is better only when it is clearly cheaper. The Novablast 5 is the better default choice if the prices are close because it uses newer FF BLAST MAX cushioning and has better current width availability.

Is the Novablast 4 good for walking?

It can work for walking if you like a soft, bouncy feel, but it is still a running shoe first. For all-day standing, some people may prefer a lower, more stable platform.

Should I buy the Novablast 4 on sale or pay more for the Novablast 5?

Buy the Novablast 4 when the discount is meaningful in your exact size. Pay more for the Novablast 5 if the price gap is small, if you need easier wide-width availability, or if you want the newest FF BLAST MAX ride.
