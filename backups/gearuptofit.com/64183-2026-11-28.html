How to Choose the Best Smartwatch for Your Child

Start with the problem you are trying to solve. A five-year-old who wants games does not need the same watch as an eleven-year-old who walks to school alone.

1. Safety need If you need location, safe zones, calling, or SOS, buy a connected GPS watch, not a toy watch.
2. Phone ecosystem iPhone families can use Apple Watch SE 3 Cellular. Android or mixed households may prefer Garmin, Fitbit, TickTalk, or Xplora.
3. Monthly cost Most GPS/calling watches need a plan. Non-connected trackers avoid monthly fees.
4. School policy Confirm whether watches are allowed, and use School Mode or Schooltime when required.
5. Charging routine If your child forgets to charge devices, Garmin vivofit jr. 3 is much easier than an LTE watch.
6. Privacy comfort Choose reputable brands with parent controls, privacy notices, updates, and clear data policies.
Simple decision rule: GPS + calling for independence, fitness tracker for habits, toy watch for fun, Apple Watch for older kids already inside the iPhone ecosystem.

Safety, Privacy, and School Rules Parents Should Check First

Connected kids’ watches can collect sensitive information such as location, contacts, messages, and sometimes voice, photos, or health/activity data. Before you buy, review the company’s privacy policy, location controls, contact approval system, data deletion options, and update history.

Red flags
  • Unknown brand with no clear privacy policy
  • No parent-approved contact list
  • No firmware update process
  • Unclear cellular plan or reseller warranty
  • Open internet or app store for young kids
Green flags
  • Parent-managed contacts
  • School mode or focus mode
  • Clear monthly plan details
  • Known brand support and documentation
  • Easy location sharing controls

Treat privacy setup as part of the purchase. Review permissions, disable unnecessary features, use only approved contacts, and teach your child when it is appropriate to call, message, share location, or use camera features.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best smartwatch for kids overall?

Garmin Bounce 2 is the best overall pick for most families because it combines location tracking, two-way calling, messaging, activity tracking, school mode, chores, rewards, and a kid-specific design.

What is the best kids smartwatch without a monthly fee?

Garmin vivofit jr. 3 is the best no-subscription fitness tracker for kids. VTech KidiZoom DX3 is the best no-subscription toy-style watch for younger children. Neither offers GPS tracking or calling.

Which kids smartwatch is best for iPhone families?

Apple Watch SE 3 GPS + Cellular is the best option for iPhone families with older kids. It supports parent-managed setup, but the parent needs a compatible iPhone and the cellular model requires a carrier plan.

Which kids smartwatch is best for video calling?

TickTalk 5 is the best choice when voice and video calling are the priority. It is designed as a child-safe phone alternative with GPS, SOS tools, school mode, and parent-approved contacts.

Are kids smartwatches safe?

They can be safe when you choose a reputable brand, use parent-approved contacts, enable school/focus mode, keep software updated, and review privacy settings. Avoid unknown cheap GPS watches with unclear data policies.

Should a 5-year-old have a GPS smartwatch?

Only if there is a clear safety need and the child can tolerate wearing and charging it. For most 5-year-olds, a non-connected option such as VTech KidiZoom DX3 or Garmin vivofit jr. 3 is more appropriate.

Can a kids smartwatch replace a phone?

For many children under 12, yes. Garmin Bounce 2, Fitbit Ace LTE, TickTalk 5, Xplora X6Play, and Apple Watch SE 3 Cellular can provide calls, messages, and location without handing over a full smartphone.

Which kids smartwatch has the best battery life?

Garmin vivofit jr. 3 has the best battery life because it uses a replaceable coin-cell battery that can last up to one year. Connected LTE watches usually need charging every day or every few days depending on use.
