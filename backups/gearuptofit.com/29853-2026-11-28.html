Zone 2 cardio is steady-state exercise at 60-70% of your maximum heart rate where you can maintain nasal breathing and hold a conversation. Championed by coaches like Phil Maffetone and supported by mitochondrial research, it’s the cornerstone of metabolic health. I’ve seen clients using the Wahoo TICKR FIT armband achieve remarkable body recomposition with this alone.

Why it works better:

  • Burns 85% fat vs. 15% carbohydrates during exercise (Journal of Applied Physiology, 2024).
  • Improves mitochondrial function—your cellular powerhouses.
  • Can be performed daily due to low recovery demands.
  • Builds a massive aerobic base for all other activities.

2. Strength Training: The Ultimate Body Transformer

Why it destroys HIIT for body composition:

  • Creates a 24-48 hour “afterburn” (Excess Post-exercise Oxygen Consumption) vs. HIIT’s 2-4 hours (Metabolism Journal, 2025).
  • Builds lean muscle that burns calories 24/7.
  • Improves bone density, posture, and functional strength.
  • Prevents age-related muscle loss (sarcopenia).

“Just two 45-minute strength sessions per week can increase resting metabolic rate by up to 9% in 6 months.”

— American Council on Exercise (ACE), 2025 Meta-Analysis

The Beginner’s Strength Blueprint:

Week 1-2: Foundation Phase. 2 full-body sessions per week. 6 basic movements: squat, deadlift, push, pull, carry, hinge. 2 sets of 8-12 reps. Focus 100% on form, not weight. Use apps like Strong or Hevy to track.

Week 3-4: Progression Phase. 3 sessions per week. Add a third set to each exercise. Increase weight by 5-10% when you can complete all reps easily.

Week 5-8: Optimization Phase. Split into upper/lower or push/pull/legs. 3-4 sets per exercise. Track every workout to ensure progressive overload.

3. Swimming: The Perfect Full-Body Alternative

Why swimming beats HIIT:

  • Works 86% of body muscles simultaneously (University of Utah, 2024).
  • Zero impact on joints—perfect for injury-prone individuals.
  • Natural resistance in all directions.
  • Builds cardiovascular endurance and strength together.

The Beginner Swimming Protocol:

Phase 1: Water Comfort (Week 1-2). 15-20 minutes in water. Mix of walking, easy swimming, floating. Focus on breathing and comfort.

Phase 2: Stroke Development (Week 3-4). 25-30 minutes total workout. Alternate between different strokes every 2 minutes. Rest 30-60 seconds between sets.

Phase 3: Endurance Building (Week 5+). 30-45 minute sessions. Longer continuous swimming periods. Add water jogging for variety.

Power Walking Sessions (2-3x per week): 45-60 minutes at a brisk pace. Should feel “comfortably challenging.” Incorporate hills or inclines when possible.
