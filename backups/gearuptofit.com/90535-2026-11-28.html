Design and First Impression

The Pegasus Trail 5 GORE-TEX looks like a shoe that understands how most people actually move.

It is not a bulky hiking boot. It is not a stripped-down trail racer. It is not a road shoe pretending to be tough. It sits in the middle: athletic enough for running, protective enough for sloppy weather, and stylish enough to wear casually.

The overall shape feels familiar if you have worn Nike Pegasus shoes before. The difference is that this version adds a more weather-ready trail personality: a waterproof upper, a protective toe area, a grippier outsole, and a more secure outdoor-ready build.

The built-in gaiter-like heel collar is a practical touch. It helps limit small stones, dirt, and trail debris entering the back of the shoe. This is not the same as wearing a full trail gaiter, but for park trails, gravel roads, wet grass, and mild dirt paths, it is a useful detail.

The best part is that the shoe does not scream “technical trail gear.” You can wear it with running shorts, hiking pants, joggers, jeans, or commuter gear and it still looks natural.

Fit and Sizing

Nike states that the Pegasus Trail 5 GORE-TEX fits small and recommends ordering a half size up.

That is the most important buying advice in this entire review.

  • Go half a size up from your usual casual shoe size.
  • Use your normal running socks when trying it on.
  • Leave a thumb’s width of space in front of your longest toe.
  • Make sure the midfoot feels secure, not squeezed.
  • Test the heel lock before committing.

This matters even more in a waterproof shoe. GORE-TEX uppers typically have less stretch than airy mesh uppers. That can make a slightly tight fit feel more noticeable during longer runs, descents, or warm conditions when your feet swell.

For trail shoes specifically, GearUpToFit’s trail running shoe fit guide recommends enough toe space for swelling and descents, a snug midfoot, and a locked-in heel. That advice applies perfectly here.

Upper: Waterproof, Protective, and Warmer Than Mesh

The upper is the main reason to choose the GORE-TEX version over the standard Pegasus Trail 5.

Nike uses a GORE-TEX Invisible Fit upper designed to keep water out while preserving a more flexible running-shoe feel. Nike also mentions waterproof protection and a 3-D printed toe cap that helps keep water away from the foot.

In real-world terms, this upper is useful for rainy runs, wet grass, puddles, damp gravel, cold-weather walks, spring and fall trail conditions, slushy sidewalks, and travel days when weather is unpredictable.

The tradeoff is breathability. Waterproof shoes almost always run warmer than standard mesh running shoes. RunRepeat’s lab review praised the Pegasus Trail 5 GTX as a reliable all-in-one option for cold, wet winter conditions, but also noted that it sacrifices breathability compared with more ventilated shoes.

That makes the buying decision simple: choose the Pegasus Trail 5 GORE-TEX if you hate wet feet. Choose the regular Pegasus Trail 5 if you run mostly in dry, warm weather.

The biggest compliment you can give the Pegasus Trail 5 GORE-TEX is that it does not feel like a waterproof hiking brick.

The ReactX foam midsole gives it a cushioned, responsive ride that feels familiar for road runners. It has enough protection for gravel and mild trails, but it is still smooth enough for pavement. That is the core of the shoe’s appeal.

Some waterproof trail shoes feel stiff and clunky. The Pegasus Trail 5 GORE-TEX feels more like a daily trainer that gained weather protection and trail traction.

That versatility matters. Most runners do not need a hyper-specialized shoe for every surface. They need a shoe that feels good for the messy middle: half road, half trail, a little gravel, a little rain, and maybe a coffee stop afterward.

Breathability: The Main Tradeoff

The main weakness is breathability.

This is not a design failure. It is the predictable tradeoff of waterproof footwear.

A GORE-TEX upper helps block water from entering, but it also traps more warmth than open mesh. On cold, wet days, that is a benefit. On hot summer runs, it can feel clammy.

The shoe is best used in rain, cold weather, wet spring conditions, damp fall training, winter walking, morning dew, mild snow, and slush.

It is less ideal for hot summer runs, humid weather, long dry trail runs, and runners with very sweaty feet.

For hot weather, the non-GORE-TEX Pegasus Trail 5 makes more sense. For wet weather, the GORE-TEX version is the better tool.

Stability and Trail Control

The Pegasus Trail 5 GORE-TEX is a neutral shoe. It does not have a medial post or traditional stability system.

On pavement, gravel, and smooth trails, it feels stable enough for most neutral runners. The midsole has a comfortable platform, and the outsole adds confidence on mixed surfaces.

On technical terrain, stability becomes less convincing. This is not a low, wide, highly planted mountain shoe. The cushioning and road-to-trail geometry make it more comfortable than precise.

Use it confidently on smooth dirt, fire roads, park trails, gravel, mild climbs, wet sidewalks, and easy hiking paths. Be cautious on off-camber trails, rooty descents, loose rock, muddy singletrack, and fast downhill technical sections.

Durability Expectations

The Pegasus Trail 5 GORE-TEX should be durable enough for road-to-trail training, walking, commuting, and light hiking.

The outsole is designed with high-abrasion rubber, which helps when the shoe sees pavement, gravel, and trail surfaces.

However, durability depends on use. Pavement wears trail lugs faster than dirt. Sharp rock stresses the outsole and upper more than smooth gravel. Waterproof uppers can last well, but they should be cleaned properly and allowed to dry naturally.

  • Rotate it with a dry-weather running shoe.
  • Do not put it in a dryer.
  • Rinse off mud after trail use.
  • Air dry away from direct heat.
  • Use it for wet, mixed, less technical terrain.
  • Avoid using it as a technical hiking boot replacement.

How to Choose the Right Size

Because Nike says this shoe fits small, do not rush sizing.

  1. Start with a half size up from your everyday shoe size.
  2. Try the shoe on with the socks you actually run in.
  3. Stand up and check for a thumb’s width of space at the front.
  4. Walk downhill or simulate a descent to check toe pressure.
  5. Make sure your heel does not lift.
  6. Confirm the midfoot feels secure but not squeezed.
  7. If your foot spills over the platform, consider a wider shoe instead.

A waterproof upper will not stretch like soft mesh. A snug fit in the living room can become an annoying fit after 45 minutes of running in wet weather.

Final Verdict

The Nike Pegasus Trail 5 GORE-TEX is one of the most useful shoes in Nike’s trail lineup because it solves a problem that many runners actually have: wet feet on mixed-surface routes.

It gives you waterproof protection without feeling like a stiff hiking boot. It has enough cushioning for pavement, enough grip for gravel and light trails, and enough style for everyday wear. That makes it more versatile than many traditional trail shoes and far more weather-ready than most road shoes.

The drawbacks are real. It is warmer than a mesh shoe. It is not built for deep mud. It is not the best choice for technical mountain terrain. And runners with wide feet should be careful with fit.

But for rainy road-to-trail runs, gravel paths, daily walking, light hiking, travel, commuting, and winter training, the Pegasus Trail 5 GORE-TEX is a strong buy.

Bottom line: Buy the Nike Pegasus Trail 5 GORE-TEX if you want a waterproof, cushioned, neutral, road-to-trail shoe that can handle real-world weather and everyday mixed-surface use.
