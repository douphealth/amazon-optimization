Design & fit: small, but helpful changes for athletes

From a fit perspective, the AirPods Pro 3 feel similar to their predecessor, but there’s two little changes that matter for sport: the new foam tips and the geometry tweaks. The foam tips provide a more secure seal for a surprising range of ear shapes. Apple also added an extra-extra-small tip, bringing the set to five sizes (XXS, XS, S, M, L). If you’ve struggled to keep earbuds in while moving, these changes will help.

That small change dramatically reduced micro-movement and prevented any ejection during hard sprints or when I splashed into water.

Heart rate sensor tech and Apple Fitness integration

Apple chose infrared optical sensors for the AirPods Pro 3 heart rate sensing — the same family of sensor technology used in most modern wrist wearables and smartwatches. This is a deliberate change from the green LED optical solution used in some other earbuds earlier this year (for example, the Powerbeats Pro 2 had green optical sensors), and it’s a better fit for the ear environment because IR penetrates tissue differently and can reduce certain motion artifacts.

Apple built the integration into the Apple Fitness app so you can record a workout using just your phone and AirPods. Once paired, your phone’s Fitness app will show additional sport profiles and let you set a goal (calories, distance, or time), choose music (a playlist or “Pick For You”), and enable Workout Buddy. Workout Buddy is Apple Intelligence-powered coaching and cues — you no longer need an Apple Watch for it to run; the AirPods can provide the heart rate feed directly to the Fitness app.

Important limitation: the heart rate data from the AirPods Pro 3 does not broadcast as a standard Bluetooth heart rate signal to third-party gym equipment (e.g., Peloton). That means you can’t pair the earbuds as an HR sensor to a bike head unit or gym display. For some people that’s a dealbreaker — for others, it’s not a problem because the Fitness app captures everything you need and third-party apps like Strava can ingest the workout afterward.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are the AirPods Pro 3 truly waterproof for swimming?

Simple. No. While they boast significant water resistance (often IPX5/IPX7), meaning they're sweat-proof and can handle splashes or light rain during outdoor activities, they are not designed for prolonged submersion like swimming.

Most people overstate 'waterproof.' All that really matters is they can endure your most intense workouts without failing. For dedicated swimming, you need purpose-built waterproof sports watches or earbuds designed for that specific environment.

How accurate is the heart rate monitoring on AirPods Pro 3 for fitness tracking?

My experience indicates it's highly accurate for an ear-based sensor. Apple has invested heavily in advanced sensors for biometric data. While chest straps remain the 'gold standard' for clinical-grade accuracy, the AirPods Pro 3 deliver reliable, real-time feedback that's more than sufficient for general fitness tracking, endurance training, and zone-based workouts. This provides a clear value proposition, especially for those seeking to monitor workout metrics without additional bulky gear.

Do AirPods Pro 3 provide enough sound isolation for a noisy gym?

Absolutely. The Active Noise Cancellation (ANC) on the AirPods Pro 3 is cutting-edge. It creates a robust sound isolation bubble, effectively blocking out most gym music, chatter, and machine noise. This allows for deep focus, enhanced motivation, and helps you concentrate on your training sessions. The premium experience extends to this ability to control your sonic environment, making them ideal for distraction-free workouts.

Can I use AirPods Pro 3 with Android devices for sports?

Yes, you can. While the seamless integration and full feature set are optimized for the iOS ecosystem, AirPods Pro 3 still offer cross-platform performance via Bluetooth. You'll get superior sound quality, reliable connection, and basic touch controls. However, advanced features like automatic device switching, Spatial Audio with dynamic head tracking, and deep customization will be limited. They still function as high-performance athletic earbuds, but you'll miss some of the 'next-gen' polish.

What about battery life for long runs or extended use?

The battery life is excellent for dedicated sports earbuds. I consistently get several hours of listening with ANC active, and the MagSafe charging case extends this significantly, providing multiple rapid charging cycles. For most long runs, gym sessions, or even ultra-marathon training (with strategic quick charge breaks), they will hold up. This extended use means less anxiety about power and more focus on your personal bests.
