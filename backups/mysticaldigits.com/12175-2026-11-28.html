Quick answer: Pythagorean numerology gives every letter a value from 1 to 9 in alphabetical order: A = 1, B = 2 … I = 9, then J starts again at 1. To find a name number, add the values of the letters in your full birth name and reduce the total to one digit, keeping 11, 22 and 33. All letters give your Expression number, the vowels give your Soul Urge, and the consonants give your Personality number. The chart and a worked example are below so you can check any calculator's result by hand.

A quick way to remember it: write the alphabet in rows of nine. Each letter's column is its number.

How to calculate your name numbers

  1. Use your full birth name, as it appears on your birth certificate, including middle names. Some readers also calculate their everyday name and compare the two.
  2. Convert each letter using the chart.
  3. Add each name separately and reduce each one to a single digit. Keep 11, 22 or 33 if they appear.
  4. Add the reduced names together and reduce again. That is the final number.

Reducing each name first, rather than adding every letter in one long sum, is the most common method. It also makes a master number visible when one name totals 11, 22 or 33. Some calculators add all letters at once. Most final results match, but master numbers can differ, which is one reason two calculators sometimes disagree.

Is Y a vowel?

This is the most common reason two Soul Urge results differ. The usual rule counts Y as a vowel when it makes a vowel sound, as in "Lynn" or "Mary". It counts as a consonant when it sounds like a consonant, as in "Yolanda" or "Bryant", where it sits beside another vowel. Pick one rule and apply it consistently. If a calculator doesn't say which rule it uses, check by hand.

Where the name comes from

The system is named after Pythagoras, the Greek philosopher whose school treated numbers as the key to understanding the world. The letter chart itself isn't found in ancient sources. It was popularized by Western writers in the early twentieth century, such as L. Dow Balliett, and later taken up by most modern numerology books. "Pythagorean" describes the tradition it draws on, not an ancient method handed down unchanged.

Common calculation mistakes

  • Using a nickname for Expression. Traditional Pythagorean practice uses the full birth name. A nickname can be read separately as a "current name" number.
  • Reducing master numbers. If a name or the final total reaches 11, 22 or 33, most practitioners keep it.
  • Mixing charts. Some calculators that call themselves "Pythagorean" use Chaldean values, and some Chaldean ones do the reverse. Check one letter: in Pythagorean, O = 6.
  • Skipping accents and hyphens inconsistently. Treat accented letters as their base letter (É = E) and ignore hyphens and spaces.

How to use the result

Name numbers are a symbolic language for reflection. They are not a measurement of character and don't predict outcomes. A useful way to read them: note where your Expression and Soul Urge differ. For example, a 7 Expression with a 3 Soul Urge could prompt the question "Do I hide a playful side behind a serious one?" Treat the answer as something to think about, not a verdict. Don't use numerology to make decisions about health, money, legal matters or relationships.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Pythagorean numerology chart?

It assigns the numbers 1 to 9 to the alphabet in order and repeats every nine letters: A, J and S are 1; B, K and T are 2; and so on up to I and R, which are 9.

Should I use my birth name or my current name?

For the core Expression, Soul Urge and Personality numbers, traditional Pythagorean practice uses the full birth name. Many readers also calculate a married name or chosen name and read it as the energy of their current identity.

Why do different calculators give me different numbers?

Usually one of three reasons: one uses the Chaldean chart, they treat Y differently, or one reduces master numbers and the other keeps them. The worked example above lets you check which is which.

Is Pythagorean numerology the same as Life Path numerology?

The Life Path number is part of the same Western (Pythagorean) tradition, but it is calculated from your birth date, not your name.
