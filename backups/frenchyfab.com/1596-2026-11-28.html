Quick answer: yes, you can take a French Bulldog out in the snow, but keep outings short and watch your dog closely. Frenchies have a short, single coat and little protection from cold. The ASPCA suggests a coat or sweater for short-haired dogs, covering from the base of the tail to the belly, and its rule of thumb is simple: if it is too cold for you, it is probably too cold for your pet. After every walk, wipe and dry the feet and belly to remove ice-melt chemicals, and keep antifreeze well out of reach, because it is lethal to dogs.

Can French Bulldogs go out in the snow?

Most French Bulldogs enjoy a short play in the snow, but they are not built for long cold outings. Their short coat gives little insulation and their compact body is close to the cold ground. Keep snow walks brief, keep your dog on a lead, and head home as soon as your dog starts to slow down, shiver, lift its paws or look for shelter. The ASPCA advises never letting dogs off lead on snow or ice, especially in a snowstorm, because they can lose their scent and become lost.

Never leave a Frenchie outside or alone in a car in cold weather. The ASPCA warns that cars can act like refrigerators and hold in the cold.

Does a French Bulldog need a coat?

For cold, wet or windy weather, usually yes. The ASPCA recommends that short-haired dogs wear a coat or sweater with a high collar or turtleneck and coverage from the base of the tail to the belly. When fitting one for a Frenchie:

  • Measure the chest at its widest point; the breed's deep chest means many standard coats are too tight there.
  • Make sure the neck and chest are not tight enough to press on the throat or restrict breathing.
  • Check that your dog can walk, sit and lie down normally.
  • Choose something that stays warm when damp, and take it off indoors so your dog does not overheat.

My French Bulldog won't wear a coat

Introduce it gradually with rewards: let your dog sniff it, then drape it over the back for a treat, then fasten one strap, building up over several days. Keep early sessions short and positive, and put it on just before something your dog loves, such as a walk.

Protecting paws from ice, salt and chemicals

The ASPCA's cold-weather advice includes:

  • Massaging petroleum jelly or a vet-approved paw protectant into the pads before going out, or using booties.
  • Removing snowballs from between the toes.
  • Washing and drying the feet and belly after every walk to remove ice, salt and chemicals, and checking for cracked pads or redness between the toes.
  • Using pet-friendly ice melts at home, and keeping de-icing chemicals out of reach.

Antifreeze: a winter emergency

Antifreeze is lethal to dogs. VCA Animal Hospitals explains that its main ingredient, ethylene glycol, tastes sweet, so dogs may drink it from spills or leaks, and that even a tiny amount can cause severe poisoning. Early signs include vomiting, lethargy, wobbliness and increased thirst and urination, progressing to seizures, coma and kidney failure. If you think your dog has swallowed any antifreeze, contact your vet or an animal poison helpline immediately. The ASPCA suggests cleaning up spills thoroughly and considering products that contain propylene glycol rather than ethylene glycol.

Keeping your Frenchie comfortable indoors

  • A warm bed off the floor and away from draughts.
  • Humidity: dry indoor heat can leave skin itchy and flaky; the ASPCA suggests keeping your home humidified and towel-drying your dog when it comes in.
  • Fewer baths: bathe as little as possible in cold spells, and ask your vet about a moisturising shampoo if needed. Keep drying the skin folds after walks and baths (see our grooming guide).
  • Indoor exercise: short training sessions, sniffing games and food puzzles keep your dog busy on cold days. See our French Bulldog games guide.

Winter feeding and weight

The ASPCA notes that pets burn extra energy staying warm and that some benefit from a little extra food in winter, along with plenty of water. Many Frenchies, though, exercise less in winter and gain weight. Weigh your dog regularly and adjust portions based on body condition; see our feeding guide.

Signs your French Bulldog is too cold

  • Shivering or trembling
  • Lifting paws, hunching or tucking the tail
  • Slowing down, reluctance to keep walking, or trying to turn home
  • Whining or seeking shelter

Bring your dog inside, dry it off and warm it gradually with blankets. If your dog is weak, very lethargic, confused or cold to the touch, contact a vet straight away.

Frequently asked questions

How cold is too cold for a French Bulldog?

There is no single safe temperature, because wind, wet and your dog's age and health all matter. Follow the ASPCA's rule of thumb: if it is too cold for you, it is probably too cold for your pet. Keep walks short and use a coat in cold, wet or windy weather.

Why are my Frenchie's ears cold?

Thin, upright ears lose heat quickly, so cool ears on their own are common in cold weather. If your dog is also shivering or sluggish, bring it inside to warm up.

How much exercise does a French Bulldog need in winter?

The Kennel Club suggests up to an hour a day for the breed. In winter, split it into shorter outings and make up the rest with indoor games.
