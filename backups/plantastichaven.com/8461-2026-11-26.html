Monstera · Watering · Root Health

Quick answer: Water Monstera when the upper part of the potting mix has dried, not on a fixed weekly schedule. Water thoroughly until excess drains, then wait until the mix partly dries again. In brighter light and warmth it may dry faster; in winter, low light, or dense soil it can stay wet much longer.

Why Monstera roots need air

Monstera is an aroid with roots that do best in an airy, well-drained mix. Constant saturation reduces oxygen and can lead to yellow leaves, drooping, root decline, and slow recovery.

What “partly dry” means

For many indoor Monsteras, the upper quarter to third of the mix drying is a useful baseline. The exact depth depends on pot size and soil structure.

Why chunky mix changes watering

A chunky aroid mix may feel dry on the surface while still holding pockets of moisture. It also drains faster than dense peat, so you need to learn the specific pot rather than copy a schedule.

Step-by-step practical instructions

Use this repeatable routine for Monstera deliciosa and similar indoor Monsteras.

Check the upper mix

Press a finger or skewer into the upper portion of the pot and confirm it has dried.

Lift or tilt the pot

A lighter pot suggests dry-down; a heavy pot suggests moisture remains.

Water evenly

Pour slowly around the pot so the whole root ball is moistened, not just one channel.

Drain completely

Let excess water leave the holes and empty the saucer.

Recheck after 20 minutes

If water collected again in the cachepot, dump it.

Adjust by season

Check more often in bright spring/summer and less often in darker winter conditions.

Common mistakes and troubleshooting

Watering every Sunday no matter what

Schedules ignore changes in light, temperature, and pot size.

Assuming droop always means thirsty

Wet soil plus droop often means root stress.

Using dense soil in a large pot

Dense soil can hold water around roots too long.

Letting a decorative pot collect water

Standing water defeats even good soil.

Troubleshooting rule: Change one variable at a time, then watch new growth. Old damaged leaves may not repair themselves, but the plant should stabilize and produce healthier growth once the root cause is fixed.

Pet safety, toxicity, and household-risk notes

Pet safety: Monstera contains irritating calcium oxalate crystals and is commonly listed as toxic to cats and dogs if chewed. Keep it out of reach and call a veterinarian or poison hotline if a pet bites or swallows plant tissue.

Place large Monsteras so pets cannot chew leaves or aerial roots. Clean fallen leaf pieces promptly.

FAQ

How often should I water Monstera?

Water when the upper part of the mix has dried. The exact interval changes with light, pot size, soil, temperature, and season.

Should Monstera dry out completely?

Avoid keeping it constantly wet, but do not repeatedly let the entire root ball become bone dry either.

Why is my Monstera drooping after watering?

Drooping after watering can mean root stress, poor drainage, compact soil, or a pot that stayed wet too long.

Is bottom watering good for Monstera?

It can work if the root ball absorbs evenly and excess water is removed, but top watering is often easier for flushing and even moisture.

What water is best for Monstera?

Room-temperature water is usually fine. If leaf tips and mineral crusts are persistent, consider filtered water and occasional flushing.
