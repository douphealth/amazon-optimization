Full New Balance 1080v15 review

The New Balance 1080 has always been the comfort shoe in the lineup: the pair you reach for when the plan says easy, steady, long, or “just get the miles done.” The 1080v15 keeps that identity, but it feels more modern. It is still soft. It is still protective. It is still made for daily use. The difference is that it now feels more alive underfoot.That matters because daily trainers have changed. Runners no longer want a soft shoe that simply absorbs impact; they want cushioning that protects the legs while still giving something back. The 1080v15 is New Balance’s answer to that shift. It brings a new Infinion midsole, a reshaped upper, a lighter-feeling ride, and enough bounce to make recovery miles less dull.

Ride feel: soft, smooth, and more energetic than expected

The first thing you notice is the softness. The 1080v15 lands gently, especially at easy pace, and it feels best when you are cruising rather than forcing the shoe to sprint. The heel feels protective, the forefoot has enough cushioning for steady mileage, and the transition from landing to toe-off is smooth.The important improvement is that the shoe does not feel like a pillow strapped to your foot. Many max-cushion trainers are comfortable for walking but sluggish once you start running. The 1080v15 avoids that problem better than expected. It is not explosive, but the Infinion midsole gives the shoe a controlled bounce that makes everyday miles feel easier.For runners building an aerobic base, this is exactly the kind of ride that pairs well with easy-effort training. Use it for relaxed miles, then dial in your pacing with the GearUpToFit Zone 2 running calculator for easy-run pacing.

Cushioning: the reason to buy the shoe

Cushioning is the 1080v15’s strongest feature. It is the reason this shoe belongs on a shortlist of premium cushioned running shoes. The midsole feels protective enough for long runs, forgiving enough for recovery days, and comfortable enough for runners who also want one shoe for walking or standing.This is especially useful if your weekly mileage includes a mix of easy road runs, treadmill workouts, weekend long runs, and general fitness walking. If you are a beginner who wants a forgiving first premium shoe, compare this review with our guide to the best running shoes for beginners who need comfort, durability, and joint-friendly cushioning.

Upper comfort: breathable, padded, and easy to live with

The upper is built for comfort rather than race-day minimalism. You get a breathable mesh feel, a padded heel area, and a tongue that helps the shoe feel secure without turning the top of the foot into a pressure point. The shape is accommodating for a daily trainer, and New Balance remains one of the better brands for runners who need multiple width options.The upper also makes the 1080v15 a strong everyday-wear shoe. It does not look like a radical super shoe. It looks premium, clean, and wearable. That is a real advantage if you want a running shoe that can handle a morning run, errands, airport walking, or a full day on your feet.

Fit and sizing: start true to size, then choose the right width

Most runners should start true to size in their normal running shoe size. The key is width. If you usually feel squeezed in the forefoot, do not automatically size up in length. First, check whether your preferred retailer has wide or extra-wide options available.A good running shoe fit should give your toes room to spread slightly, hold the midfoot securely, and prevent heel slip without needing to crush the laces. For long runs, your feet will swell, so the toe box should not feel tight when standing still. If you regularly train for different distances, pair this fit advice with our guide to the best running shoes for different distances from 5K training to marathon mileage.

Stability: stable enough for neutral runners, not a stability shoe

The New Balance 1080v15 is a neutral shoe. That means it does not use a traditional medial post or strong motion-control structure to correct overpronation. The platform feels comfortable and reasonably planted for a soft shoe, but runners who need firm guidance should look at a stability model instead.Neutral runners, mild supinators, and runners who simply want soft protection should feel at home here. But if your ankles collapse inward, your old shoes show heavy medial wear, or you have been prescribed support shoes by a clinician or specialty running store, the 1080v15 may not provide enough structure.

Outsole and durability: built for road miles

The outsole is designed for pavement, sidewalks, track warmups, treadmill belts, and general road-running conditions. The rubber coverage protects high-wear areas while keeping the shoe from feeling unnecessarily heavy. Grip should be fine for normal dry roads and gym use, but this is not a technical trail shoe.If you split your running between indoor and outdoor training, the 1080v15 can work well in both environments. For training structure, see our treadmill workouts for beginners that build fitness safely and our treadmill vs outdoor running comparison for choosing the right surface.

Performance: daily miles first, speed second

You can pick up the pace in the 1080v15, but speed is not its main personality. It is best at easy pace, steady aerobic running, recovery mileage, and longer efforts where comfort matters. For strides, short pickups, and relaxed progression runs, it can handle the job. For intervals, race workouts, and fast tempo sessions, a lighter and firmer shoe will feel sharper.That is not a weakness; it is a category choice. The 1080v15 is a daily trainer. It is supposed to make the bulk of your mileage feel better. In a smart shoe rotation, it handles the comfort work while a faster trainer handles speed days.

Walking and all-day comfort: one of its biggest strengths

Many runners also need shoes for walking, travel, work breaks, and active recovery. The 1080v15 is excellent there. The same cushioning that protects you during easy runs also makes the shoe comfortable for standing and walking. New Balance 1080v15 vs 1080v14: what changed?

The biggest change is the move toward the new Infinion midsole feel. The 1080v14 was already a soft, comfort-first daily trainer, but the 1080v15 feels more modern and lively. It keeps the protective identity of the 1080 line while adding a smoother, bouncier character.The upper also looks more sculpted and contemporary. Instead of feeling like a simple padded trainer, the v15 has a cleaner performance silhouette. The result is a shoe that still works for easy miles but feels less old-school and more competitive in the current premium daily trainer market.

Upgrade recommendation: Upgrade from the 1080v14 if you want a lighter-feeling, bouncier, more modern daily trainer. Stay cautious if you loved the older, softer, sink-in Fresh Foam X sensation and dislike more energetic foam.

New Balance 1080v15 FAQ

Is the New Balance 1080v15 good for long runs?

Yes. The New Balance 1080v15 is a strong long-run shoe because it combines soft cushioning, a smooth neutral ride, and enough bounce to keep steady mileage comfortable.

Is the New Balance 1080v15 a stability shoe?

No. The 1080v15 is a neutral running shoe. It can feel stable enough for many neutral runners, but it is not designed as a motion-control or high-support stability shoe.

Does the New Balance 1080v15 fit true to size?

Most runners should start true to size in their normal running shoe size. If your forefoot needs more room, check wide or extra-wide options before sizing up in length.

Is the New Balance 1080v15 good for walking?

Yes. Although it is built as a running shoe, the soft cushioning, breathable upper, and comfortable heel make it a strong walking and everyday comfort shoe.

Can I use the New Balance 1080v15 for marathon training?

Yes, especially for easy runs, recovery days, and long runs. For race day or faster marathon-pace workouts, many runners may prefer a lighter tempo trainer or carbon-plated racing shoe.

What is the difference between New Balance Fresh Foam X 1080 V15 and the 1080v15?

Some retail listings may use the Fresh Foam X 1080 V15 naming, but the defining update for the 1080v15 is the Infinion midsole technology, which is designed for enhanced cushioning, energy return, and long-lasting performance.

Is the New Balance 1080v15 good for heavy runners?

It can be a good option for heavier neutral runners who want shock absorption and long-run comfort. However, heavier runners who need strong support should consider a stability shoe instead.

Is the New Balance 1080v15 good for plantar fasciitis?

The 1080v15 offers soft cushioning and comfort, which some runners may appreciate, but shoe choice for plantar fasciitis depends on your foot mechanics, support needs, and clinical history. Ask a qualified clinician or specialist running store if you have recurring pain.
