25 pirate adventure writing prompts

Historical pirates

  1. In 1718, a ship's cook learns the captain plans to accept a royal pardon and abandon the crew who refuse it.
  2. A young woman disguises herself as a sailor to join a pirate crew, and the quartermaster recognises her from her home port.
  3. A captured navigator is the only person aboard who can read the stolen charts, and she decides which way the ship will sail.
  4. A pirate crew votes to replace its captain in the middle of a storm.
  5. A merchant ship's surgeon is forced to serve pirates and must decide whether to treat the man who sank his ship.
  6. Two rival captains agree to share a hiding place for one season, and neither trusts the other.

Treasure and maps

  1. A map tattooed on a dying sailor's back is only half the route. The other half is on his twin brother.
  2. A treasure chest is finally opened, and inside is a letter addressed to the person who opened it.
  3. Three crew members each hold one piece of a map and must stay alive and together to find the hoard.
  4. A treasure buried two hundred years ago is found by a teenager on a beach holiday, and someone else has been searching for it for decades.
  5. The treasure turns out to be cursed in a very practical way: anyone who spends it is recognised immediately.
  6. A pirate hides her gold somewhere only her daughter could find, using clues from a bedtime story.

Mutiny, loyalty and betrayal

  1. A new crew member is a spy for the navy, and he starts to like the people he is meant to betray.
  2. The ship's carpenter discovers someone has been drilling small holes in the hull at night.
  3. A pirate captain must choose between saving her crew and saving her brother, who sails for the enemy.
  4. The crew is starving, the captain has hidden food and the first mate finds it.
  5. A cabin boy overhears a plot against the captain and has one night to decide whose side he is on.

Fantasy and science-fiction pirates

  1. Sky pirates raid flying merchant ships powered by trapped storms.
  2. A pirate crew sails the rivers of the underworld, robbing souls of their memories, until one memory fights back.
  3. Space pirates capture a cargo freighter and find the cargo is a sleeping colony of ten thousand people.
  4. A ship made from the bones of a sea dragon begins to remember its life.
  5. A crew of pirate cats sails between the docks of a great port city, stealing fish from the fishmongers' guild.
  6. Time pirates steal famous objects from history and must return one before history notices.
  7. A pirate radio station broadcasts from a boat just outside the law's reach, and the authorities finally come for it.
  8. A mermaid hires a pirate crew to recover something humans stole from her kingdom.

How to plan a pirate adventure

  1. Choose the prize. Gold, a person, a map, a ship, freedom. Everyone aboard should want it for a different reason.
  2. Build the crew. A captain, a quartermaster, a navigator and a newcomer give you authority, rules, skill and an outsider's view.
  3. Use the sea. Wind, tide, storms, calms and disease were real threats. They make good obstacles.
  4. Set the rules. Many historical crews sailed under written articles that set shares of plunder and rules of conduct. Your crew's rules can drive the plot when someone breaks them.
  5. End with a choice. Keep the treasure or the friend, the ship or the crew.

Real history worth checking

The period often called the ‘golden age of piracy’ is usually dated to roughly the 1650s to the 1720s and centred on the Caribbean, the American coast and the Indian Ocean. Famous names from the period include Blackbeard, who died in 1718, and Anne Bonny and Mary Read, who were tried in Jamaica in 1720. Check dates and details against a museum or academic source before you use them, because pirate history is full of later legend.

Stories to study

  • Treasure Island by Robert Louis Stevenson (1883): the map, the cook and the divided crew.
  • Peter Pan by J.M. Barrie (play 1904, novel 1911): a pirate captain as a theatrical villain.
  • Captain Blood by Rafael Sabatini (1922): a doctor turned pirate.
  • Pirates of the Caribbean: The Curse of the Black Pearl (2003): curses, mutiny and shifting loyalties.

Frequently asked questions

What makes a good pirate story?

A prize everyone wants for different reasons, a crew with divided loyalties and a sea that creates real danger. The best pirate stories are about loyalty as much as treasure.

Were pirate crews really democratic?

Some were. Historical accounts describe crews that elected captains and signed articles setting out shares and rules. Practice varied, so check sources for the crew and period you are writing about.

Can pirate stories work for younger readers?

Yes. Focus on maps, puzzles, teamwork and clever escapes, and keep violence off the page. The treasure and fantasy prompts adapt especially well.

How accurate does a historical pirate story need to be?

Accurate enough that readers who know the period are not pulled out of the story. Check ships, weapons, ports and dates, and be careful with legends presented as fact.
