Quick answer: feed a French Bulldog a complete and balanced dog food suited to its life stage, in measured portions. Start with the feeding guide on the food label, then adjust up or down based on body condition: you should be able to feel the ribs easily, like the back of your hand. Puppies are generally fed 2 to 3 measured meals a day and most adults do well on two. Keep treats to no more than 10% of daily calories. Weight matters more in this breed than most, because obesity raises the risk of breathing problems.

What do French Bulldogs eat?

French Bulldogs do not need a breed-specific food, but they do need a diet that is complete and balanced for their life stage. VCA advises checking the label for a nutritional adequacy (AAFCO) statement that says the food meets the requirements for your dog's life stage. The WSAVA Global Nutrition Committee also suggests asking manufacturers practical questions, such as who formulates their diets and what quality control they use, before choosing a brand. Our French Bulldog nutrition guide covers choosing a food in more depth.

Many owners are drawn to raw, grain-free or home-made diets. These carry extra risks if they are not properly formulated; if you want to go that route, work with a veterinary nutritionist (see our guide to homemade dog food for French Bulldogs).

How much should a French Bulldog eat?

There is no single number of cups or grams that suits every Frenchie, because calorie needs change with age, activity, neuter status and the calorie density of the food. The AKC's advice is to start with the feeding guidelines on the food package and expect some trial and error.

  1. Find your dog's ideal weight with your vet, rather than using its current weight if it is overweight.
  2. Use the label's daily amount for that ideal weight and split it into meals.
  3. Weigh the food on a kitchen scale; cups are surprisingly inaccurate for small dogs.
  4. Check body condition every two to four weeks and adjust the amount in small steps.

For more detail on portions, see how much French Bulldogs should eat.

Check body condition, not just the scale

VCA describes a simple rib test: feel your dog's ribs just behind the front legs. If they feel like the back of your hand, body condition is good. If they feel like your knuckles, your dog is too thin; if you cannot feel them easily, your dog is carrying too much fat. VCA defines dogs as overweight at 10% to 20% above their ideal weight and obese at more than 20% above it.

Weight matters particularly for French Bulldogs. A Royal Veterinary College study found that obesity further increases the risk of brachycephalic obstructive airway syndrome (BOAS) in flat-faced dogs, on top of the risk from a short muzzle. Our weight management guide has a step-by-step plan.

Feeding schedule by age

  • Puppies: measured amounts at regular times, generally 2 to 3 meals a day, adjusted to growth and body condition. Keep to a growth or all-life-stages food until your vet advises switching. Our puppy nutrition guide covers the switch to adult food.
  • Adults: two measured meals a day suit most French Bulldogs and make it easier to spot a change in appetite.
  • Seniors: needs vary; some older dogs need fewer calories, others lose weight or muscle. Regular weigh-ins and vet checks guide changes (see our senior care guide).

Treats

VCA recommends that treats and snacks make up no more than 10% of a dog's daily calories. For example, if your dog's target is 500 kcal a day, about 450 kcal should come from its main food and no more than about 50 kcal from treats. Use small pieces, count training treats as part of the budget, and avoid foods that are toxic to dogs, such as grapes, onions, chocolate and anything containing xylitol (see our list of foods French Bulldogs cannot eat).

Practical tips for flat-faced eaters

  • Slow down fast eaters with a slow-feeder bowl or puzzle feeder, which also adds enrichment.
  • Use a shallow, wide bowl that suits a short muzzle.
  • Change foods gradually over about a week to avoid stomach upsets.
  • Feed away from heat and hard exercise; let your dog settle after walks before a meal.

When to talk to your vet

  • Your dog is losing weight, or looks thin despite eating well
  • Appetite changes suddenly, or your dog stops eating for more than a day
  • Ongoing vomiting, diarrhoea, gas or itchy skin that might be food-related
  • Your dog is gaining weight even on measured portions

Frequently asked questions

How many times a day should a French Bulldog eat?

Puppies generally 2 to 3 measured meals a day; most adults do well on two.

What do breeders feed French Bulldogs?

Practices vary. What matters is that the food is complete and balanced for the puppy's life stage. Ask your breeder what the puppy eats and keep it the same for the first days at home, then change gradually if needed.

My French Bulldog always seems hungry or looks thin. What should I do?

Check body condition with the rib test and weigh your dog. If it is thin despite eating the label amount, or its appetite has changed, see your vet to rule out parasites, digestive disease or other illness before increasing food.
