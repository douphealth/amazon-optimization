What is a coming-of-age story?

A coming-of-age story follows a young person through a defining experience that changes their understanding of identity, belonging, responsibility, love, independence, or moral choice.

75 coming-of-age writing prompts

Identity and self-discovery

  1. A teenager who has always been “the responsible one” does something reckless on purpose, just to find out who she is when nobody is counting on her.
  2. A quiet student discovers he is genuinely talented at something his friends think is embarrassing.
  3. A star athlete suffers an injury that ends her season and has to figure out who she is without the sport everyone praised her for.
  4. A young artist turns down a safe scholarship in a subject she hates to pursue a future her parents call foolish.
  5. A sixteen-year-old finds out he was adopted from a letter he was never supposed to read.
  6. A girl who translates for her immigrant parents at every appointment realizes she has never been allowed to speak for herself.
  7. A teen learns that the grandfather everyone calls a hero did something shameful during the war.
  8. A boy changes his name when his family moves to a new city and has to live with the person he invented.
  9. A teenager raised in a strict religious community starts to doubt and has no one safe to tell.
  10. A student with a stutter is chosen to give the graduation speech by a teacher who thinks it will help.
  11. A teen whose life has been documented online since birth by a parent’s blog asks to have it all taken down.
  12. A girl who has always dressed the way her older sister did decides, on the first day of high school, to stop.
  13. A gifted kid skipped ahead two grades realizes he is lonely, not special.
  14. A teenager comes out to one person, and that person tells someone else before she is ready.
  15. A teen keeps a forbidden hobby, such as music, boxing or poetry, secret from his family until it becomes the only thing keeping him going.

Friendship and belonging

  1. Two best friends since kindergarten realize in their last summer together that they want completely different lives.
  2. A teen must choose between joining the popular group and staying loyal to the friend they are making fun of.
  3. A friendship breaks over one public mistake: a screenshot, a joke, a secret told at a party.
  4. The new kid is adopted by a friend group that turns out to need someone to blame.
  5. A teenager protects a friend who has done something wrong and slowly realizes the friend would not do the same for her.
  6. Three friends make a pact at twelve to meet again at eighteen. Only one of them remembers.
  7. A girl’s best friend starts dating her older brother, and suddenly she is on the outside of both relationships.
  8. A boy discovers that the friend group chat he was removed from is still active, and they are talking about him.
  9. Two friends compete for the same spot on a team, and the one who wins it did not play fair.
  10. A teenager befriends an elderly neighbor and learns more from her than from anyone her own age.
  11. A friend group splits when one member’s family suddenly becomes wealthy.
  12. A boy has to decide whether to tell a teacher that his best friend is in trouble at home.
  13. A teen who has always been the funny one in the group is the only one who notices that a friend is not okay.
  14. A summer camp friendship that felt like it would last forever does not survive the first text exchange back home.
  15. A teenager realizes her closest friendship has always been one-sided and decides whether to say so.

Family and home

  1. An older sibling leaves for university, and the youngest has to take over the role of family peacemaker.
  2. A teen becomes responsible for an adult problem (rent, a parent’s illness, a younger sibling) long before she is ready.
  3. A family moves to a new town where no one knows about the scandal, and a teen has to decide whether to keep the secret.
  4. A father and son who only talk about sport are forced to spend a week together after a family emergency.
  5. A teenager reads her mother’s diary from when she was the same age and does not recognize the person in it.
  6. Parents announce their divorce on the day of their daughter’s big performance.
  7. A boy discovers that his family’s business, which he was expected to inherit, has been failing for years.
  8. A teen who resents her strict grandmother is sent to live with her for a summer.
  9. A family rule that has never been explained turns out to be about something that happened before the protagonist was born.
  10. Twins who have always been treated as a unit apply to different universities, and only one gets in.
  11. A teenager caring for a younger brother realizes she has never had a childhood of her own.
  12. A parent remarries, and the protagonist suddenly shares a bedroom with a stepsibling who wants the same things.
  13. A teen returns to her parents’ home country for a family funeral and does not speak the language well enough to grieve with her cousins.
  14. A boy finds out his older brother, whom he idolizes, has been lying to the whole family.
  15. A daughter has to choose between staying near her struggling family and taking an opportunity far away.

First love and heartbreak

  1. A first crush reveals a family rule the protagonist never knew existed: who they are allowed to love.
  2. Two teens fall for each other during the one summer they both work at the same beach café, knowing one leaves in September.
  3. A girl writes love letters for her friends for money, then gets a request to write one to herself.
  4. A teen realizes he is in love with his best friend the day she tells him she is moving away.
  5. A first relationship ends badly in public, and the whole school takes sides.
  6. Two rivals in debate class are forced to be partners and discover they agree on almost nothing except each other.
  7. A teenager meets someone online and finally meets them in person, and they are not who she imagined, in a good way.
  8. A boy has to decide whether to go to prom with the person everyone expects or the person he actually likes.
  9. A girl’s first heartbreak happens at the same time as her parents’ divorce, and nobody notices hers.
  10. A teen falls for someone from the family her own family has been feuding with for years.
  11. A first kiss happens at the wrong moment, in front of the wrong person.
  12. Two exes are the only two people from their school accepted into the same summer program.
  13. A teen keeps a relationship secret because of what her community would say, and the secret starts to cost both of them.
  14. A boy realizes that the person he has a crush on is only interested in him because of who his father is.
  15. A long-distance first love tries to survive a year of time-zone differences, missed calls and changing lives.

School, work and the wider world

  1. A summer job at a factory teaches a teenager a harder lesson than anything at school.
  2. A scholarship interview asks a question that forces a student to reveal a truth she has hidden from everyone.
  3. A school election exposes the class divisions everyone pretended were not there.
  4. A teen’s first job introduces her to a manager who abuses his power, and she has to decide whether to report him.
  5. A shy student speaks up in class at exactly the wrong moment and becomes the center of a controversy.
  6. A mistake made online follows a teenager into school, into his job interview, and into his family.
  7. A first failure becomes public: a rejected application, a lost competition, a failed exam everyone knows about.
  8. Graduation week forces an impossible choice between a promise to a friend and an opportunity that will not come again.
  9. A road trip with two friends becomes a test of independence when the car breaks down in a town none of them know.
  10. A teen volunteers at a hospital to pad her university application and meets a patient who changes her plans.
  11. A student discovers that a beloved teacher has been inflating grades, including hers.
  12. A teenager organizes a protest at school and has to face what happens when adults take it seriously.
  13. An exchange student spends a year abroad and comes home to find she no longer fits in either place.
  14. A boy working at his uncle’s garage discovers the business is involved in something illegal.
  15. On the last day of school, a student finally says what she has been thinking for four years.

A five-step framework for coming-of-age stories

  1. Pressure: Give the protagonist a choice they are not ready for.
  2. Belonging: Define the group, family, or identity they fear losing.
  3. Mistake: Let them act from need, pride, fear, or loyalty.
  4. Recognition: Show the moment they understand the cost.
  5. New self: End with a changed decision, not a speech about growth.

Coming-of-age stories to study

  • The Catcher in the Rye by J.D. Salinger (1951): a teenager’s voice and alienation carry the whole novel.
  • To Kill a Mockingbird by Harper Lee (1960): a child’s understanding of justice changes over one trial.
  • The Perks of Being a Wallflower by Stephen Chbosky (1999): friendship, first love and trauma told through letters.
  • The House on Mango Street by Sandra Cisneros (1984): identity, family and place in short vignettes.
  • Lady Bird (2017): a mother-daughter relationship and a final year of high school.

Frequently asked questions

What is a coming-of-age story?

A story that follows a young person through an experience that changes how they understand themselves, their relationships or the world. The change usually comes from a choice with a real cost, such as leaving, telling the truth, or losing a friendship.

How do you write a coming-of-age story?

Give the protagonist a pressure they are not ready for, a group or person they fear losing, and a mistake made out of need, pride or loyalty. Then show the moment they understand the cost and end with a changed decision rather than a speech about growth.

What themes are common in coming-of-age fiction?

Identity, belonging, friendship, family expectations, first love, loss of innocence, independence and the first encounter with injustice. The prompts on this page are grouped around five of them.

How old should a coming-of-age protagonist be?

Most are between about 12 and 19, but the genre is defined by the transition, not the age. A story about a 25-year-old leaving home for the first time can still be coming-of-age.

How do I avoid clich&#233;s in a coming-of-age story?

Make the setting, family and community specific, avoid a wise adult who explains the lesson, and let the protagonist’s growth come at a price. A small, concrete decision is more convincing than a dramatic transformation.
