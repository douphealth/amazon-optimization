Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best pot size for a spider plant?

Choose a pot 1-2 inches larger than the root ball. This prevents overwatering while allowing growth. For mature plants, 8-10 inches works well. Upgrade gradually to avoid stress.

Do you water a spider plant from the bottom or the top?

Water from the top to ensure even distribution, but let excess drain fully. Bottom watering suits self-watering pots, keeping foliage dry. Aim for soil to dry slightly between sessions—spiders tolerate drought better than soggy roots.

Are terracotta pots good for spider plants?

Yes, terracotta pots for spider plants excel due to porosity. They promote quick drying and root health. Ideal for overwaterers, though they may need more frequent watering in dry homes.

How often should I repot my spider plant?

Repot every 1-2 years or when roots crowd. Spring is best. Use this repotting spider plant guide: fresh soil, slightly larger pot. Watch for signs like roots escaping drainage holes.

Can I use plastic pots for spider plants?

Plastic pots for indoor spider plants are fine if they have drainage. They’re lightweight and affordable. Ensure good airflow to prevent moisture buildup—add perlite to soil for extra drainage.

What soil mix is best with new pots?

Spider plant soil and potting mix should be well-draining: equal parts potting soil, perlite, and peat. This pairs perfectly with any pot material. Refresh during repotting for nutrient boost.
