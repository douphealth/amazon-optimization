Clear definition

AI consciousness fiction explores whether an artificial system can have awareness, identity, memory, preference, suffering, moral status, or personhood, and how humans react when a machine appears to cross that boundary.

Practical framework
  1. Premise: Define what the AI can do that changes human expectations.
  2. Evidence: Show what makes characters believe or doubt consciousness.
  3. Stake: Name who gains, loses, or becomes responsible.
  4. Choice: Force a decision that cannot be solved by more processing power.
  5. Aftermath: Show how people reinterpret the machine and themselves.
Step-by-step practical instructions
  1. Pick a prompt with real tension: Choose a prompt that already contains a decision, secret, risk, or contradiction.
  2. Define the protagonist: Name what the character wants, what they fear, and what they misunderstand at the beginning.
  3. Add setting and pressure: Give the scene a concrete place, deadline, witness, obstacle, or social cost.
  4. Write the first turning point: Draft the moment when the character makes a choice that cannot be fully undone.
  5. Revise for causality: Check that each scene happens because of a decision, not because the plot needs it to happen.
