Quick answer: chewing is normal dog behaviour. The ASPCA explains that puppies chew to relieve teething pain, and adult dogs chew to keep their jaws strong and teeth clean, to relieve boredom, and to ease mild anxiety or frustration. A French Bulldog that chews furniture, wood or blankets usually needs three things: appropriate chew toys, a dog-proofed home, and more exercise and mental work. Chewing mainly when left alone can point to separation anxiety.

How to stop a French Bulldog chewing furniture

  1. Dog-proof the house. Put shoes, clothes, cables and valuables out of reach until chewing is under control.
  2. Offer a variety of chew toys sized for a Frenchie, and rotate them so they stay interesting.
  3. Supervise and redirect. If you catch your dog chewing something off-limits, calmly swap it for an appropriate toy and praise the dog for chewing that. Punishment after the fact does not teach anything.
  4. Use confinement when you cannot watch, such as a crate or a dog-proofed pen with toys (see our crate training guide).
  5. Add exercise and mental work suited to the breed: short walks at cool times, sniffing games, food puzzles and a few minutes of training. French Bulldogs overheat easily, so do not use hard exercise to tire them out.
  6. Consider taste deterrents on furniture, alongside the steps above, not instead of them.

Chewing when left alone

The ASPCA notes that dogs who chew to relieve the stress of separation anxiety usually chew only, or most intensely, when left alone, and often show other signs such as whining, barking, pacing, restlessness and house soiling. This needs a separation training plan, and sometimes help from your vet. Our separation anxiety guide sets one out.

Sticks, wood and leaves

Chewing sticks and wood can leave splinters wedged in the mouth; VCA lists wood fragments and plant matter among the common foreign bodies that make dogs drool and paw at the mouth. Swallowed pieces can also cause stomach or intestinal problems. Swap sticks for a safe chew toy, and see your vet if your dog is drooling, gagging, pawing at its mouth, vomiting or off its food after chewing wood.

Chewing hands and puppy mouthing

Puppies explore with their mouths. Redirect mouthing onto a toy, pause play when teeth touch skin, and reward gentle behaviour. Our guide to stopping French Bulldog biting covers this step by step.

Blankets and fabric

Some dogs lick, suck and chew fabric. The ASPCA notes that some experts link this to early weaning, and that if it goes on for long periods and the dog is hard to distract, it may have become compulsive. In that case, look for help from a veterinary behaviourist or a certified behaviour professional. Watch for swallowed fabric, which can cause a blockage.

When to see a vet

  • Your dog has swallowed something it should not have, or is vomiting, straining or off its food
  • Chewing starts suddenly in an adult dog, or comes with drooling or pawing at the mouth, which can mean dental pain
  • Chewing happens mainly when your dog is alone and comes with other signs of distress
  • Your dog is licking or chewing its own paws or skin, which often points to itching or allergy

Frequently asked questions

Why does my Frenchie chew everything?

Most often teething in puppies, or boredom and too little mental exercise in adults. If chewing happens mainly when you are out, consider separation anxiety.

When do French Bulldogs stop chewing?

The intense teething phase usually ends by about six months, but chewing remains a normal behaviour for life; the goal is to direct it onto the right things.

Should I let my French Bulldog chew sticks?

It is safer not to. Sticks can splinter and lodge in the mouth or be swallowed. Offer a safe chew toy instead.
