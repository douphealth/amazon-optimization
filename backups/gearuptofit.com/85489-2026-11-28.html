Use cases: where the Takumi Sen 11 shines

I see the Adidas Takumi Sen 11 in two primary roles:

  1. 5K / 10K race shoe: This is the Takumi Sen’s most natural habitat. It’s light, fast, and nimble. If you’re frequently racing short road distances or doing track efforts, this shoe will reward quick turnover and aggressive pacing.
  2. Speed-focused marathon training tool: I don’t race a ton of 5Ks on the roads, but I do marathon workouts—threshold mile repeats, progression miles, fast long-run segments—and the Takumi Sen 11 is my go-to for those sessions. It brings enough durability and cushioning to tolerate repeated hard reps while giving me the pop I want without beating up my legs like a spike would.

I even raced a 5,000-meter track event in the Takumi Sen 11. For me, spikes for a 20-minute effort felt excessive; the Takumi gave me enough grip and responsiveness to enjoy the race while staying within the limits my body prefers at this stage.

Evolution of the Takumi: what changed from the older models

To understand the 11, you have to understand the lineage. Earlier Takumi models—like the Takumi Sen 8 I still own—were designed with a small forefoot landing pad and a large medial side cutout. The idea was simple: force toe-biased running, reduce forefoot mass, and encourage a fast, front-of-foot push where the shoe bends easily and snaps back for an energetic toe-off.

Over the years that forefoot pad has increased in size, and the medial cutout has migrated backward and shrunk. In the Takumi Sen 11, the cutout is essentially gone; you get just a peekaboo window to view the EnergyRods. Functionally there’s still a small notch, but it’s the smallest in the lineup so far.

That’s a notable change in philosophy. I think Adidas is trying to harmonize the Takumi experience with the Adios Pro 4 and other modern racers—so you get a more robust forefoot platform while maintaining the signature low-feel and responsiveness of Takumi.
