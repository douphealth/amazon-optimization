Safety instructions that apply across the guides

Cleanup: do not sweep or vacuum untreated rodent waste. Wear rubber or plastic gloves, wet the waste thoroughly with a bleach solution or an EPA-registered disinfectant and let it soak for 5 minutes or according to the disinfectant label. This is a safety reminder, not the full cleanup procedure. [4] Heavy contamination and ventilation-system contamination need additional precautions or professional help. See CDC cleanup guidance for the complete procedure.

Trapping: CDC recommends snap traps of the appropriate size and advises against glue and live traps. Keep devices away from children and pets, follow the instructions and inspect traps daily. [3] See CDC trapping guidance.

Exclusion: use the repair method appropriate to the opening. CDC describes small-hole filling and more substantial materials for larger openings; [5] a single foam or mesh product is not a universal building repair. See CDC exclusion guidance. Get qualified advice around electrical, gas, venting and fire-rated assemblies.

When to involve a professional

Ask for help when contamination is heavy or inaccessible, ventilation systems are affected, damaged wiring is suspected, species identification remains uncertain, or new signs persist despite your actions. Shared buildings may require coordinated inspection rather than work in just one unit.

Request a written scope covering inspection, trapping, exclusion, cleanup, follow-up and any warranty conditions. Local labor, access and repair needs determine the quote; this hub does not publish an invented universal cost or clearance timetable. Compare what is included, not merely the headline price.

Organize the observations into a plan

The free Mouse Control Planner arranges next steps around the signs and locations you report. It cannot diagnose an infestation, confirm species, estimate population, calculate disease risk or guarantee a clearance date. The source guides remain available without taking the quiz.

Does a week with no sightings prove the home is clear?

No single observation guarantees lasting control. Keep a dated record of trap checks and newly observed signs, inspect repairs and revisit food access. No sightings is not the same as a complete inspection.

Should I start with a repellent?

Do not substitute a scent product for evidence-based inspection, control and repair. Use the repellent guide above to understand the limitations before spending money.
